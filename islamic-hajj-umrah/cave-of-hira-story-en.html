Cave of Hira: The Story of the First Revelation and How to Climb It Today

August 22, 2026 8 min read

The Cave of Hira is a small opening near the summit of Jabal al-Nour in northeast Makkah, about 4 km from Masjid al-Haram. It is where the first revelation came to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. Climbing to it today takes roughly one to two hours on foot depending on your fitness, and the best time to go is before sunrise or after Asr.

This guide covers both sides: the story as narrated in the authentic sources, briefly, and a practical plan for the visit from your hotel to the top and back.

Where exactly is the Cave of Hira?

The cave is on Jabal al-Nour, a sharp-peaked mountain on the northeast side of Makkah, roughly 634 meters high. The cave itself is a narrow gap in the rock with its opening facing north. Only a handful of people can sit inside at once, and when you do, you are facing the Kaaba.

Getting to the foot of the mountain takes a few minutes by taxi or ride-hailing app from the Haram. At the base is the Hira Cultural District, where the climbing route starts. The path has been upgraded with signage, rest points and safety services, and climbs may be organized in groups depending on the season and crowds.

How long does the climb take?

  • If you walk regularly: about an hour.
  • If you don't, or you have kids with you: two hours or more.
  • Coming down: quicker, but harder on the knees. Take it slower than the way up; a light walking stick helps a lot.

The route is stone steps and rocky trail, and it narrows in the last third. When the big boulders appear, you are not there yet. The cave is right below the summit, and you will recognize it by the people gathered around it.

Best time to visit the Cave of Hira

Start an hour before sunrise, or after Asr prayer. Climbing at midday in Makkah's heat is genuinely exhausting, and it is the number one thing that ruins the visit.

Heads up: in the hot months the trail may close during the middle of the day. In the 2025 season, for example, it was closed daily from 11 am to 4 pm from the start of Dhul-Hijjah to the start of Rabi al-Awwal. These hours change from season to season, so confirm them before you set out.

A sample day

The morning plan (best for most people): go to bed early, leave the hotel about forty minutes before Fajr, pray at the Haram, then head to the foot of the mountain and start climbing at first light. You will reach the top before the heat builds at nine, be back for a late breakfast around ten, and still have the rest of the day for tawaf and rest.

The evening plan: climb after Asr and come down before Maghrib. Descending in the dark is harder, so bring a small flashlight or check that the path is lit in your season.

Either way, treat the climb as a half-day trip, not a quick outing. Don't book anything tight afterwards; the tiredness tends to hit an hour or two later.

What to bring

  • Water: 1.5 to 2 liters per person, even in winter.
  • A light snack: dates or a banana. Nothing more.
  • Proper shoes: soles that grip rock, not the light sandals you wear at the Haram.
  • A hat or head cover if it is hot.
  • Your hotel details: save the name and address in our Return to Hotel card. After half a day of walking, it is easy to forget the street name.

Don't climb alone in the dark, and tell someone where you are going and when you expect to be back. For prayer times during the trip, use our Prayer Times tool.

The night revelation began

Before prophethood, the Prophet ﷺ would spend many nights alone in the Cave of Hira, worshipping and reflecting away from the noise of Makkah. He would take provisions with him, then return to his wife Khadijah to gather more for the next stay. The books of Seerah place the first revelation in Ramadan, when he was forty years old.

On one of those nights, the angel Jibreel came to him and commanded him to read. The Prophet ﷺ told him that he could not read. The angel held him in a tight embrace, released him and gave the command again, and this happened three times. Then the angel recited the first words of the Quran ever revealed: the opening of Surah Al-Alaq, a command to read in the name of God the Creator. The story is recorded in the two most authentic hadith collections.

The Prophet ﷺ returned home with his heart trembling and asked his family to wrap him up. He told Khadijah what had happened and that he feared for himself. She did not panic. She reassured him with what she knew of his character: a man who kept ties with his relatives, carried the burdens of the weak, gave to those in need, honored his guests and stood by people in hardship would never be let down by God. She then took him to her cousin Waraqah ibn Nawfal, a man who knew the earlier scriptures, and he told him that what he had seen was the same angel who had come to Musa.

The first word of this message was a command to read, given to a man who could not read, in a place with nothing of worldly grandeur about it. That is worth holding in mind when you stand at the opening.

Why Ramadan is special at Hira

Because revelation began in Ramadan, and the Quran itself names Ramadan as the month in which it was revealed. That is why many pilgrims make a point of climbing during Ramadan. If you are fasting, climb after Asr with enough time to be down before iftar, and never climb at midday while fasting in the heat.

Expect crowds at the opening in Ramadan and peak seasons; people sometimes queue to reach it. Don't push. If it is packed, sit on a nearby rock overlooking Makkah. The view from there is just as good as from the cave itself.

Is visiting the Cave of Hira a Sunnah?

No. Visiting the cave is not part of Hajj or Umrah. The Prophet ﷺ did climb Mount Hira after prophethood with some of his companions, as narrated in Sahih Muslim, but there is nothing reported from him encouraging people to seek out the cave for worship or to pray there. Most scholars consider visiting historical sites to reflect and take lessons permissible, but there is no evidence of any special merit in praying or making dua in the cave itself.

So if you climb, climb to reflect. Read some Quran if you like, but don't take stones or write on the rock. And if you can't make the climb, you have lost nothing; a pilgrim who reflects deeply during tawaf may take away more than someone who climbed, took a few photos and came down. For the finer points of the ruling, ask a qualified scholar.

Cave of Hira vs. Cave of Thawr

Many visitors mix the two up, and some only realize after an hour of walking.

Make sure your map app or driver is heading to Jabal al-Nour.

Climbing with kids or older family members

It is doable, with three conditions: children who can manage a long uphill walk, plenty of water, and a cool time of day. Hold their hand on the narrow sections and around the boulders. A child who sees the place with their own eyes will remember the story better than from any book.

Anyone with knee, heart or blood pressure problems should check with their doctor before climbing, and nobody should push themselves just to reach the top.

Common mistakes that spoil the visit

  1. Climbing at midday: the most common mistake, and the easiest to avoid.
  2. Bringing less than a liter of water: you lose more fluid than you think.
  3. Rushing the descent: rocky steps are riskier on the way down than on the way up.
  4. Mixing up Hira and Thawr: double-check the destination before you leave.
  5. A tight schedule afterwards: leave time to rest, especially if you are traveling the same day.

Frequently asked questions about the Cave of Hira

Where exactly is the Cave of Hira?

On Jabal al-Nour (the Mountain of Light) in northeast Makkah, about 4 km from Masjid al-Haram. The climb starts at the Hira Cultural District at the foot of the mountain.

How long does it take to climb to the Cave of Hira?

About an hour if you walk regularly, and two hours or more if you don't or have children with you. Coming down is quicker.

What is the best time to visit the Cave of Hira?

An hour before sunrise, or after Asr. In summer the path may close around midday, so check the opening hours before you go.

Is visiting the Cave of Hira a Sunnah?

No. It is not part of Hajj or Umrah. Visiting it to reflect on its history is permissible, but there is no special merit attached to praying or making dua in the cave itself.

What is the difference between the Cave of Hira and the Cave of Thawr?

Hira is on Jabal al-Nour in northeast Makkah, where the first revelation came. Thawr is on Jabal Thawr in south Makkah, where the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr hid for three nights during the Hijrah.

Is the climb suitable for families?

Yes, if your children can handle a long uphill walk. Bring plenty of water, go when it is cool, and keep them close on the narrow sections.
