Umrah and Ziyarah

Crowd Times and the Best Time for Tawaf

November 10, 2026 11 min read

The difference between an exhausting Umrah and a calm one is often just timing. The same rituals and the same distance, yet tawaf at a crowded hour can take twice as long with pushing and heat, while tawaf at a quiet hour gives you room to focus and make du'a. If you are with children or older parents, timing becomes a matter of safety, not only comfort.

This guide pulls together what is known about crowd patterns at Masjid al-Haram by hour, day and season, based on published Ministry of Hajj and Umrah guidance and the repeated experience of pilgrims, and turns it into a practical plan for choosing your Nusuk slot and leaving your hotel.

The basic rule: crowds follow the prayers

The single most important thing to understand is that the Haram breathes to the rhythm of the five prayers. About an hour before each obligatory prayer, people start pouring in, the rows and courtyards fill, and the way down to the mataf courtyard sometimes closes. Right after the prayer comes a big wave of people starting tawaf, because many wait for the prayer to end before beginning their Umrah. Movement then eases gradually over the next hour or so.

So the worst moment to start tawaf is the minutes right after the prayer ends, and the worst moment to enter is just before the adhan. The best approach is to begin once the post-prayer wave has settled, or to use the long gaps between prayers that are far apart.

The best times of day for tawaf

Ministry of Hajj and Umrah guidance, as summarised by Wego, notes that the period from after Fajr until late morning is consistently the least crowded, and that late night between Isha and the early hours is the second-best window, which is especially useful in summer when daytime heat is intense. Khaleej Times also published a breakdown of the peak and off-peak periods identified by the Saudi authorities.

These are general patterns, not guaranteed times. In Ramadan the picture flips: after Maghrib and Taraweeh is extremely crowded, while after Fajr and late morning become relatively calm because people rest after suhoor.

Days of the week: Friday and weekends

Friday is one of the busiest days at Masjid al-Haram. The mosque starts filling hours before the Friday prayer and many gates close early. If you are performing Umrah, avoid Friday morning unless you start very early after Fajr and finish before the mosque fills.

Weekends, meaning Thursday night through Saturday, also bring many pilgrims driving in from inside the Kingdom and from the Gulf states. If your trip is flexible, do your Umrah tawaf midweek and keep the weekend for prayers and du'a rather than the rituals.

Seasons: when is the Haram calmer?

The busiest periods are Ramadan, especially the last ten nights, the weeks before Hajj, and school and Eid holidays. By contrast, the months after Hajj are usually calmer, and the weather improves from October to March as temperatures drop. We cover months and costs in detail in our guide to Umrah in off-peak seasons.

Summer is a special case. The Haram is less crowded than in Ramadan, but the heat in the outer courtyards is severe and walking from the hotel at midday is draining. If you perform Umrah in summer, do tawaf at night or after Fajr, drink plenty of water, and carry an umbrella for the courtyards.

Book your Umrah permit in a quiet window

The Umrah permit on Nusuk is booked as a time slot, and a common mistake is to grab the first slot available and then discover it falls after Maghrib on a Thursday night. Take an extra minute when booking and pick a slot in one of the calm windows from the table above. Published reports confirm that visitors are expected to book an Umrah slot in the app before heading to Masjid al-Haram.

If your trip includes Madinah, remember that the Rawdah needs an entirely separate permit. We explain the steps, timings and the instant track in our guide to booking the Rawdah on Nusuk.

Tawaf on the upper levels

If the mataf courtyard is full, the alternative is the first floor or the roof. Tawaf there is valid and usually less crowded, but each circuit is much longer because the circle is wider, so the whole tawaf takes considerably more time. This option is excellent for people who can manage the walk and for those using a wheelchair or electric cart, since cart routes are usually on the upper levels.

A common mistake is to start tawaf in the courtyard, get tired, and move upstairs halfway without knowing how to continue. If you expect to tire, start upstairs from the beginning, or choose a quiet time for the courtyard. To see which gates lead to the mataf or to the upper levels, read our guide to the gates of Masjid al-Haram.

A calm day plan for families

Here is a tried plan for a family with children or older relatives, built around the morning window:

  1. Sleep early the night before, and lay out ihram clothes, water and a small bag before bed.
  2. Pray Fajr in or near the Haram, then have a light breakfast.
  3. Start tawaf about an hour after Fajr, once the post-prayer wave has eased.
  4. Go straight to Sa'i after tawaf and its two rak'ahs, with a short break if anyone needs it.
  5. Finish before Dhuhr and head back to the hotel to rest through the hottest hours.

Before you set out, agree on a meeting gate and its number, and give every child a card with the hotel name and a phone number. For more on keeping little ones and older parents comfortable, see Umrah with kids and Umrah with elderly parents.

Signs that tell you to wait

  • A red light above the gate you are heading for means that side has reached capacity.
  • The way down to the mataf is closed, which means the courtyard is full. Either wait or do tawaf upstairs.
  • The adhan is less than half an hour away, so your tawaf may be interrupted by the prayer. It is better to pray first and wait for the wave to settle.
  • A family member is tired. That matters more than any schedule. A later tawaf is better than one under pressure.

Waiting one hour at the hotel is often better than two hours pressed in a dense crowd. To understand where your hotel sits relative to the gates, see our Makkah hotels guide.

Heat, water and rest during tawaf

Crowds and heat make each other worse. Inside the covered mataf areas and on the air-conditioned levels conditions are gentler, but the outer courtyards in summer are very hot and the floor reflects the sun. Do your walking across the courtyards early in the morning or at night, and avoid leaving the hotel at midday unless you must.

  • Zamzam water is available from coolers throughout the mosque, so you do not need to carry much, but take a small bottle for the walk from the hotel.
  • A small bag with a shoe bag, tissues and a power bank is enough. Large bags are a burden in the crowd.
  • A break between tawaf and Sa'i with children and older relatives is not a shortcoming. Sitting for ten minutes after the two rak'ahs of tawaf restores energy for the rest of the rites.
  • Comfortable shoes for the walk to the Haram, and socks for tawaf if the floor is cold on winter nights.

If anyone feels dizzy or badly exhausted, stop immediately and ask the nearest staff member for help, as there are first-aid points close by inside Masjid al-Haram. Your family's health comes before finishing a circuit on schedule.

Extra tawaf and the farewell tawaf

Many pilgrims want to do extra tawaf during their stay after the Umrah itself. That tawaf is not tied to your Umrah permit slot, so you are free to pick the calmest time for it: after Fajr on a midweek day is usually best. If you want to bid farewell to the Kaaba before you travel, plan it well ahead of your departure rather than in the final hour, so the crowd and your bus or flight time do not squeeze you at once.

A good habit is to write out your whole stay on one sheet: Umrah day, days for prayers in the Haram, the transfer day to Madinah if you have one, and travel day. Seeing the days laid out lets you give each activity its calmest window instead of chasing the crowd day by day.

Disclaimer: This is general guidance. Crowd patterns are estimates and change with the season and events. The reference for permits and timings is the Nusuk app and the Ministry of Hajj and Umrah, and always follow the staff in the Haram. For religious rulings, consult qualified scholars.

Frequently asked questions

What is the least crowded time of day at Masjid al-Haram?

Ministry of Hajj and Umrah guidance points to the period from after Fajr until late morning as consistently the least crowded, followed by the late night hours after Isha.

When are the crowds at their peak?

Just before and right after the obligatory prayers, on Thursday night and Friday, in the last ten nights of Ramadan, and during school holidays and weekends.

Is tawaf on the first floor or the roof valid?

Yes, tawaf on the upper levels is valid and usually less crowded, but each circuit is much longer, so it suits people who can walk further or who use a wheelchair or cart.

Do I need a permit to perform Umrah?

Yes. An Umrah permit with a time slot is booked on the Nusuk app before you go to Masjid al-Haram, and choosing a quiet slot from the start makes your whole day easier.

Which months are best for a less crowded Umrah?

The months after the Hajj season such as Muharram and Safar are usually calmer than Ramadan and the holiday months, and the weather is milder from October to March.
