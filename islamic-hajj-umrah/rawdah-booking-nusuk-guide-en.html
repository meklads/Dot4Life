Booking the Rawdah on Nusuk

November 10, 2026 10 min read Reviewed by Ibrahim Meklad

The Rawdah is the area between the pulpit and the Prophet's chamber inside the Prophet's Mosque in Madinah, and for most visitors it is the single place they most want to pray in. The space is small and demand is enormous, so prayer there is organised through timed permits issued only by the Nusuk app. If you arrive without a permit you will not be let in, however long you wait at the doors.

This guide covers what you actually need on the ground: how to book, the announced timings for men and women, the instant track that allows repeat visits, and how to handle the day of your slot so you do not lose it. The details come from official announcements reported in the Saudi press, and the final word is always what the app shows you at the moment you book.

What the Rawdah is and why you need a permit

The Rawdah is a defined part of the Prophet's Mosque, marked by green carpet and white decorated pillars, and it holds a limited number of worshippers at a time. Before permits, the crowding at its entrances was severe and older visitors and women were often pushed. The current system admits visitors in groups, gives each group roughly ten minutes to pray and then moves it out through the other side, as described in Al Watan's report on the group system.

Keep two things separate. Praying in the Prophet's Mosque itself needs no booking at all and you can enter at any time. Praying inside the boundary of the Rawdah is what needs the permit. Many visitors confuse the two: some think they are barred from the whole mosque, while others assume that being inside the mosque gets them into the Rawdah.

The permit is personal and free. There is no official fee for the Rawdah, and anyone offering you a slot for money is exploiting your need. Even Umrah companies that organise group visits book through the same platform.

How to book on the Nusuk app

The steps below reflect the current version of the app. Button names may shift slightly with updates.

  1. Update the app to the latest version before you travel. Many "no slots showing" problems come from an old version.
  2. Log in with the account linked to your national ID, residency or visa. Visitors on a tourist or Umrah visa register with their passport and visa number.
  3. Add companions from your family to your account if you will manage their bookings. Each person needs their own slot.
  4. Go to Services and choose the Rawdah visit or prayer service. Note that this is separate from the Umrah permit for Makkah. Mixing the two up is one of the most common first-time mistakes.
  5. Pick the visitor, then the date, then an available time window. Full windows cannot be selected.
  6. Confirm the booking and wait for the confirmation message.
  7. Save the permit from My Bookings or My Permits and check that it shows a QR code, the date and the time. Take a screenshot as a backup.

A practical tip: do not keep hammering refresh inside the calendar screen. Go back one step and enter again, which reloads availability more cleanly. New slots also appear when other people cancel, so trying at spaced-out times during the day works better than trying non-stop.

Rawdah timings for men and women

Men and women enter in separate time windows and through separate entrances. The schedule announced by the authorities and published by Okaz was as follows:

DayMenWomen
Saturday to Thursday2 AM until Fajr, then 11:20 AM until IshaAfter Fajr until 11 AM, then after Isha until 2 AM
Friday2 AM until Fajr, then 9 AM until IshaAfter Fajr until 9 AM, then after Isha until 2 AM

These hours are adjusted in peak seasons, especially Ramadan and the Hajj period, so treat the table as a planning aid rather than a final appointment. The binding time is the one printed on your permit. If your family includes men and women, plan the visit as two separate windows and agree on a meeting point outside the mosque after each one.

The instant track: visiting more than once

A regular advance booking allows one permit every 365 days per person, a rule the Ministry of Hajj and Umrah announced at the start of 2024. Nusuk later added a service called the instant track, aimed at people who are physically in the central area around the Prophet's Mosque.

Through the instant track you book the nearest available slot without a long wait. Slots refresh roughly every twenty minutes, and you can repeat the visit throughout the year as long as you are near the mosque, according to Al Watan and Sayidaty citing the ministry.

How do you use it in practice? If you could not find an advance slot before you travel, do not worry. Once you are at your hotel near the Prophet's Mosque, open the app while you are inside the central area and try the instant track at different times of day. Location services on your phone need to be on so the app can tell that you are close by. This is exactly why a nearby hotel pays off, and we compare the options in our guide to Madinah hotels near Masjid an-Nabawi.

On the day: what to do step by step

A permit alone is not enough if you arrive late or get lost on the way. Here is a simple plan for the day of your slot:

  • Charge your phone before you leave and carry a power bank. The permit lives on your screen and is useless if the phone dies.
  • Arrive early, around twenty to thirty minutes before your time, because reaching the gathering point inside the mosque takes a while on busy days.
  • Follow the signs and the staff to the Rawdah gathering point for men or for women. Do not rely on memory from a previous visit, since routes change.
  • Have the QR code ready on your screen before the checkpoint, together with your ID or passport.
  • Inside the Rawdah, pray two rak'ahs and make your du'a calmly. Remember each group has about ten minutes, so keep photos to a minimum and do not crowd others.
  • After you exit, head to the meeting point you agreed with your family.

If you are with an older relative, ask the staff for the easiest route and make sure their permit is the first one ready. If you miss your slot, the permit generally expires with its window, so book a new one or try the instant track.

An important warning: never share your verification code or account details with anyone who offers to book for you. The account is tied to your identity and any misuse falls on you.

Tips for families

If you are visiting Madinah with children or older parents, arrange the bookings around each person's energy rather than the time that suits you best. The women's morning window after Fajr suits those who sleep early, and the night windows after Isha suit those who tire in the daytime heat.

Age rules are applied inside the app for each profile. If the app will not allow a booking for a child, do not try to bring them in. Instead, decide who will stay with them in the mosque outside the Rawdah boundary. Older children benefit from a short explanation before the visit: where you are going, why the place is special, and that the time is short and needs calm.

If this is your family's first trip, also read our Umrah with kids guide and Umrah with elderly parents, which cover comfort and getting around. For the whole mosque, its gates and courtyards, see the complete guide to Masjid an-Nabawi.

Before you travel: a short checklist

  • Install and update Nusuk, and register your account and your companions' accounts a few days before you fly.
  • Try an advance booking early, since slots fill fast in busy seasons.
  • If you find nothing, plan to use the instant track after arrival and choose a hotel in the central area.
  • Write down the family meeting point for after the Rawdah and save it on every phone.
  • Carry a power bank and your ID, and stick to the early arrival time.

If you are moving between Makkah and Madinah, the order of your days matters. Many families put Madinah at the end of the trip so the Rawdah becomes a calm close after the effort of Umrah. If you want a less crowded time for the whole journey, see our guide to Umrah in off-peak seasons.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Rawdah permit free?

Yes. The permit is issued free of charge through the Nusuk app and there is no official fee. Anyone asking for money to get you a Rawdah slot is not an authorised channel.

How often can I visit the Rawdah?

A regular advance booking is limited to one permit every 365 days per person. The Nusuk instant track lets people who are near the Prophet's Mosque book the nearest available slot and visit again during the year.

What are the Rawdah timings for men and women?

The most recently announced schedule: men from 2 AM until Fajr and from 11:20 AM until Isha; women from after Fajr until 11 AM and from after Isha until 2 AM, with different hours on Friday. The time printed on your own permit is what counts.

Does every family member need a separate permit?

Yes. Each visitor needs their own appointment, although one phone can manage the family's bookings by adding companions inside the app.

What should I do if my permit does not show after booking?

Open My Bookings or My Permits, pull down to refresh, then log out and back in. If it is still missing, contact Nusuk support with your booking reference.
