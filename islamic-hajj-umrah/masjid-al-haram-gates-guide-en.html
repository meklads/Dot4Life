Masjid al-Haram Gates: Which One to Use

November 10, 2026 10 min read Reviewed by Ibrahim Meklad

The first thing that confuses most pilgrims at Masjid al-Haram is not the rituals but the gates. The mosque is vast, its gates are many and look alike, the courtyards around it are wide, and after a crowded prayer people pour out in every direction. Many families get separated at exactly this moment, and some spend an hour looking for a child or an older parent because they left through two different gates.

This guide gives you only the gates you actually need to know: the five main gates and their numbers, which ones take you straight to the mataf, the gates for Umrah pilgrims and for people with disabilities, and a simple plan so your family stays together. The figures come from announcements by the General Authority for the Affairs of the Two Holy Mosques as reported in the Saudi press.

How many gates does the Haram have?

According to Al Khaleej, citing the General Authority for the Two Holy Mosques, Masjid al-Haram has 243 gates, of which 68 are currently in use. The authority has assigned 17 gates for Umrah pilgrims, 42 gates for worshippers coming to pray, and 9 entrances for people with disabilities.

These numbers have a practical meaning: not every open gate serves every purpose. At peak times the management directs Umrah pilgrims to specific gates that lead to the mataf, and directs worshippers to gates that lead to the upper floors and the expansions. If you are told at a gate that it is for worshippers only, do not argue. Ask for the nearest gate for Umrah pilgrims.

The five main gates and their numbers

In an awareness infographic reported by Sabq, the authority explained that the mosque has five main gates, each marked by two minarets above it so it can be recognised from a distance:

GateNumberPractical note
King Abdulaziz Gate1Faces the Clock Towers and is one of the gates with direct access to the mataf.
Al-Fath Gate45On the northern side, close to the Mas'a.
Al-Umrah Gate62On the north-western side, and listed among the gates for people with disabilities.
King Fahd Gate79On the western side, with direct access to the mataf.
King Abdullah Gate100Part of the third Saudi expansion on the northern side.

The golden rule: memorise the number, not just the name. Names blur together for children and older people, while numbers are printed clearly above the gates and on signs, and they are easier to report if someone gets lost. Take a photo of the sign of the gate you entered by. That single photo can save you half an hour of searching.

Where to enter for tawaf

If you are arriving for Umrah and want the shortest route to the mataf courtyard, published announcements point to King Fahd Gate, King Abdulaziz Gate and the Salam basement for direct access, as reported by Sayidaty. Other gates listed for Umrah pilgrims include Al-Umrah Gate, Al-Salam Gate and Gate 145, along with the bridges such as the Ajyad, Safa, Marwa and Shubaikah bridges, served by lifts and escalators.

Keep in mind that access to the mataf changes with the crowd. At peak times the way down to the mataf courtyard may close temporarily and pilgrims are sent to the first floor or the roof. This is not your mistake; it is crowd safety. Tawaf on the upper levels is valid, but the distance is much longer, so factor that in if you are with an older person or a child.

After tawaf you head to the Mas'a. Sa'i starts at Safa and ends at Marwa, and leaving from the Marwa end puts you on the north-eastern side of the mosque. If your hotel is on the Clock Tower side you will need to walk back across the courtyards, so do not assume that the nearest gate after Sa'i is the nearest one to your hotel.

Gates for people with disabilities and older pilgrims

Gates named in official announcements for people with disabilities include King Fahd Gate, King Abdulaziz Gate, Al-Umrah Gate and Gate 153. Entrances to the upper levels include the Al-Arqam Gate lifts, the Al-Umrah Gate lifts and the Ajyad stairway lifts, according to Al Khaleej.

If you have a wheelchair, ask the staff at the gate about the wheelchair route before you go in, because wheelchair tawaf is usually done on dedicated paths on the upper levels. Make sure whoever pushes the chair knows the number of the gate you will exit by, since leaving a crowded gate with a wheelchair after prayer is exhausting.

A tip for anyone travelling with older parents: do not enter just before an obligatory prayer, because that is when some sides of the mosque reach capacity. Go in early and sit, or wait until movement eases after the prayer. We break down these times in our guide to crowd times and the best time for tawaf.

Green and red lights above the gates

The gates of Masjid al-Haram have guidance signs that light up green when worshippers can enter and red when that side has reached capacity, as Okaz reported. If you see red, do not stand at the gate waiting. Move straight to another gate. Standing in front of a closed gate only adds to the crowd and will not open it.

In the big seasons such as Ramadan, some gates close well before the prayer. The answer is to arrive very early, or to pray in the outer courtyards, which are carpeted and prepared for prayer and connect to the rows inside.

A plan so your family does not get separated

Most separations in the Haram do not happen during tawaf but at the exit after prayer. So set a simple plan before you go in:

  1. Pick one meeting gate by name and number, ideally a main gate with two minarets that is easy to spot.
  2. Choose a point outside the gate, not inside, such as a particular pillar or the gate's number sign on the outside, because the inside fills with movement.
  3. Agree on a time, for example twenty minutes after the prayer, and whoever arrives first waits and does not move.
  4. Give children a card with the hotel name, a parent's phone number and the meeting gate number, in a pocket it cannot fall out of.
  5. Teach the child to go to the nearest security officer or staff member if lost, and to stay where they are.

Choosing a hotel by gate

When you compare hotels, do not only ask how far they are from the Haram. Ask: to which gate? A hotel three hundred metres from a worshippers' gate may be further in practice than one five hundred metres from King Abdulaziz or King Fahd Gate, because worshippers' gates do not take you to the mataf. We discuss this trade-off in our comparison of hotels near the Haram and budget options and our Makkah hotels guide.

If your hotel is further out, in areas such as Aziziyah or Shawqiyah, you will usually arrive by bus or taxi at a stop near one of the courtyards. In that case save the location of the drop-off point as well as the gate, because your journey back starts at the stop.

Common gate mistakes and how to avoid them

  • Relying on memory alone: after two hours inside the Haram, gates and courtyards look the same to everyone. A photo of the gate sign and its number beats any memory.
  • Leaving shoes at one gate and exiting by another: shoe racks are numbered near the gates, and anyone leaving from a different side spends a long time searching. Carry a small cloth shoe bag and keep your shoes with you.
  • Leaving with the first wave after prayer: the worst crush at the gates is in the first minutes after the prayer ends. Sit for ten minutes of dhikr, then leave when movement is lighter.
  • Splitting the family at the entrance: if men and women enter through different gates for organisational reasons, agree on the exit gate, meeting point and time before you separate.
  • Standing in the flow: waiting right in the gate opening blocks people coming in and out. Wait to one side, away from the stream.

Remember that phone signal can be weak inside the mosque and on the lower levels, so do not rely on calls alone to meet up. An agreed gate and time is what brings the family back together when the network drops or a battery dies.

Disclaimer: This is general guidance compiled from published announcements. Gate assignments, openings and closures change with the season and the crowd by decision of the General Authority for the Affairs of the Two Holy Mosques, so always follow the staff on site. For religious rulings, consult qualified scholars.

Frequently asked questions

How many main gates does Masjid al-Haram have?

Five main gates, each topped by two minarets: King Abdulaziz Gate (1), Al-Fath Gate (45), Al-Umrah Gate (62), King Fahd Gate (79) and King Abdullah Gate (100).

Which gate is closest to the mataf for Umrah?

Direct access to the mataf courtyard is through King Fahd Gate, King Abdulaziz Gate and the Salam basement. Follow the staff, because routes change with the crowds.

Can I leave by any gate?

Usually yes, but it is better to leave by the gate you agreed with your family, since exiting elsewhere can put you on a side of the mosque far from your hotel.

Which gates suit wheelchair users and people with disabilities?

Gates officially listed for people with disabilities include King Fahd Gate, King Abdulaziz Gate, Al-Umrah Gate and Gate 153. Ask the staff for the nearest lift or ramp.

What do the red and green lights above the gates mean?

Signs at the gates show green when there is room to enter and red when that side of the mosque has reached capacity. If you see red, head to another gate.
