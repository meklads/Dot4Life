Hijri New Year: How to Teach Your Children the Meaning of the Migration and the Start of the Year

🕌 Islamic📅 2026-06-21

When your child asks, "Why do we have a new year in Muharram?" you are standing at a golden teaching moment that does not come often. Here is the short answer: introduce your child to the meaning of the Hijri New Year by connecting it to the story of the Prophet's migration from Mecca to Medina, explaining that Muslims made this great event the start of their calendar, then turn the occasion into a simple, loved family activity rather than letting it pass as a number on the calendar. The Hijri calendar begins from the year of the Prophet's migration to Medina in 622 CE, and the Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab set it to begin from the month of Muharram (Britannica).

This guide explains the migration story in a child's language, why this event was chosen as the start of the year, and practical ideas to make the occasion an experience your little ones love.

What Does the Hijri New Year Mean?

The Hijri New Year is the first day of Muharram, the first month of the Hijri calendar that Muslims follow. But this calendar is not just numbers for counting days. Its beginning carries a great story, the Prophet's migration. When you explain this to your child, do not start with dry dates, but with the story that grips their heart.

The idea you want the child to grasp is simple: Muslims chose to begin their year from the memory of an event that changed the course of Islam, the move of the Prophet and his companions from Mecca to Medina. This choice teaches the child that every nation has defining moments worth remembering, and that the start of our year is tied to a meaning, not merely a celebration.

The Migration Story in a Child's Language

Tell your child the story as if it were a real adventure, because it is. In Mecca, the Prophet and his companions faced much harm because of their call to Islam. So God commanded His Prophet to migrate to Medina, where its people awaited to welcome and support him. The Prophet set out with his companion Abu Bakr on a journey full of danger, and they hid in the cave of Thawr while those pursuing them were very close.

Let the child live the moment: the calm in the cave, the Prophet's trust in his Lord, and his arrival at last in Medina, where the Ansar received him with joy and songs. This story holds lessons the child grasps with the heart before the mind: patience under harm, reliance on God, and the importance of brotherhood and support. Tell it with warmth and enthusiasm, and it will stay in their memory longer than you imagine.

Why Was This Event Chosen as the Start of the Year?

Years after the migration, the Muslims needed a unified system to date their letters and dealings. So the Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab consulted his companions, and they agreed that the Prophet's migration would be the start of the calendar, because it was the greatest defining event in the history of Islam at the time, and Muharram was made the first of the months (Britannica).

Explain the point to your child: they did not choose the Prophet's birth or another event, but the migration, because it was the beginning of building the Muslim community in Medina. This teaches the child that the lesson is not in mere remembrance, but in the beginnings that shape the future. And since this is a historical matter, take its details from trusted sources, not from this simplified overview alone.

Make It a Chance to Plant Values, Not Just a Celebration

The Hijri New Year is more than a date on the wall. It is an entry point to teach the child values that stay. From the migration story they learn patience as they see how the Prophet and his companions endured harm. They learn reliance on God as they see their trust in His support. And they learn the value of brotherhood as they see the Ansar open their homes to the migrants.

Invest the occasion in a calm conversation with your child about their year: What did they learn? What do they wish to improve in themselves? This simple reflection plants the habit of self-review and aspiring to better. This way the new year passes not as a number, but as a station where the child pauses to grow a little.

The Hijri Calendar in Our Daily Life

Many children know the Gregorian date from school and the phone, but may not connect to the Hijri calendar except in Ramadan and Eid. The Hijri New Year is a chance to teach your child that we have our own calendar that orders our seasons and acts of worship. Explain that Ramadan, Hajj, and the Eids are all reckoned by the Hijri calendar, so they realize it is not just old numbers but a living part of their life.

Make the connection practical. Show your child how the Hijri date differs from the Gregorian, and why Ramadan moves forward about eleven days each year because the lunar year is shorter. This small observation makes the child notice their calendar and look at the crescent with interest. Over time the calendar becomes present in their awareness rather than foreign, a lesson tying the child to their identity.

The Role of Example in Anchoring the Occasion

A child absorbs from their parents more than from any lesson. When they see you care about the Hijri New Year and speak of it with love, they learn it matters. And when the occasion passes you by without notice, they learn the opposite. You do not need a grand celebration, but sincere attention shown in a kind word, a calm talk, and a simple activity you share with them.

Make the occasion a family moment that brings you together, not a duty placed on the child alone. Sit together, tell the story, reflect on your year as a family. This family presence is what anchors the occasion in the child's heart, making it a memory they will repeat with their own children when they grow.

A Practical Example From a Gulf Home

Umm Muhammad wanted to make the Hijri New Year special for her three children without fuss. On the night of Muharram she gathered them after Isha, dimmed the extra lights and lit a soft glow, and told them the migration story as if it were an adventure. She paused at the cave scene and asked the children: how would you have felt? They engaged and imagined the moment with their hearts.

The next day each child drew a scene from the story, and they hung the drawings on the fridge. Then the mother sat with each child for two minutes, asking what they learned in their year and what they wished for. It was neither costly nor long, but the children remembered it for a long time and asked to repeat it the following year. The lesson is simple: sincere attention and a warm story make a greater mark than any costly celebration.

FAQ

When does the Hijri New Year begin?

The Hijri year begins on the first day of Muharram, the first month of the Hijri calendar. Its Gregorian equivalent shifts each year because the Hijri year is lunar, about eleven days shorter than the Gregorian.

Why does the Hijri calendar begin from the migration, not the Prophet's birth?

Because the companions, led by Umar ibn al-Khattab, saw that the migration was the defining event that began the building of the Muslim community. The historical details are taken from trusted sources.

How do I simplify the migration story for a young child?

Tell it as an adventure with a journey, a cave, and friends waiting. Focus on the feelings and values, not the dates. Repeat it in different ways through drawing and play so it sticks in their memory.

Is there a special celebration for the Hijri New Year?

It is not a religious Eid like Fitr and Adha, but there is no harm in using it as a simple family educational occasion. For any religious ruling about celebrating, refer to qualified scholars.

What values can a child learn from the migration?

Patience under harm, reliance on God, the value of brotherhood and support, and the importance of beginnings. These are values the child grasps from the story with the heart before the mind.

Should I teach my child a specific supplication for the new year?

The Takeaway

The Hijri New Year is an irreplaceable chance to connect your child to their history and values. Start with the migration story as a warm adventure, explain why this event was chosen as the start of the year, and turn the occasion into a simple, loved family activity. Above all, make it a moment for the child to reflect on their year and grow a little in awareness. That way Muharram becomes a meaning in their memory, not just a number.
