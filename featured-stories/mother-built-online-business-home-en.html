Mother of Three Built Online Business from Home

Jul 1, 2026 10 min read

Summary: Noura Al-Ghamdi, a Saudi mother of three, turned a simple sewing hobby into a thriving online business that now serves customers across the Gulf. Her journey from a small home project to a full digital brand shows what is possible when determination meets smart online tools. This is her story, shared with practical insight for any mother considering the same path.

The quiet part of the evening had settled over the house. Outside, the summer heat of Jeddah pressed against the windows. Noura sat at her dining table, the laptop screen glowing in the dim light, her youngest asleep in the room next door. A year ago, the same spot held schoolbooks and tea cups. Tonight, it held an order summary showing more than two thousand riyals in sales from the past week.

She had not planned to start a business. What began as a favour for a friend - sewing a custom abaya with a modern cut - turned into a request from another friend, then another, until her phone would not stop buzzing with messages. That was when she realised: the skills she used as a hobby could become something real if she took them seriously.

The Starting Point: From Hobby to First Sale

Noura had always sewn. As a teenager in Khobar, she altered her own clothes and made small gifts for family. After marriage and the arrival of her three children, the sewing machine sat in a corner, used only for mending. It was not until a close friend saw a dress Noura had made for her daughter and asked for one too that the thread began to unspool in a new direction.

"I made the dress as a gift," Noura told us. "She insisted on paying. That was the first time I earned money from something I loved doing."

The amount was small - 150 riyals. But the feeling was not. She created an Instagram account and posted photographs of her work against a plain white wall. Within a month, she had sixteen followers and three orders. Her husband, an engineer, supported her but warned her not to expect too much. "He was being realistic," she says with a laugh. "I was being stubborn."

The Turning Point: Going Digital

Orders trickled in through direct messages, but Noura knew she could not scale her business on Instagram alone. A cousin who ran a small online shop recommended she set up a simple website. Noura spent two weeks learning the basics of Shopify through free YouTube tutorials, building her store late at night after the children went to bed.

"I made many mistakes. The photos were too dark. The descriptions were too long. My first website looked terrible," she admits. "But I kept going because every mistake taught me something."

Her breakthrough came when she added a digital catalogue with clear pricing and standard sizes. Orders from outside Jeddah started arriving. Women in Riyadh, Dammam, and even Bahrain sent messages asking for her designs. What had been a local hobby became a regional business without Noura ever leaving her home.

Balancing Motherhood and Business: The Real Challenge

Building the business was hard. Balancing it with motherhood was harder. Noura wakes before her children, at 5:30 AM, to answer messages and pack orders. During school hours, she focuses on sewing and production. The afternoons belong entirely to her children - homework, meals, playtime. She returns to work after 9 PM, when the house is quiet.

"There were days I wanted to stop. My youngest was teething, an order was late, and I had not slept properly in a week," she says. "But I reminded myself why I started. I wanted to contribute to my family and build something my children would be proud of."

The structure she built is simple but effective: mornings for customers, afternoons for family, evenings for growth. She does not aim for perfect balance. She aims for consistent effort, and that has been enough.

The Financial Impact: From Savings to Steady Income

Within eighteen months, Noura's online business was generating a monthly income that matched her husband's engineering salary. She reinvested a portion into better equipment - an industrial sewing machine, quality fabrics, professional packaging. The rest went into household savings and her children's education fund.

Her story is not unique in its broad outlines. Across Saudi Arabia, women are turning home-based skills into digital businesses. The Ministry of Commerce reported that over seventy thousand commercial registrations were issued to women in 2025 alone, with home-based online businesses forming the fastest-growing segment. What sets Noura apart is her deliberate approach to growth: slow, steady, and grounded in the reality of her family responsibilities.

Practical Lessons for Mothers Starting Out

Noura offers direct advice for Saudi mothers considering their own online business:

  • Start without waiting for the perfect plan. She began with one dress and an Instagram account. The plan developed as she went.
  • Use free resources first. YouTube tutorials, free website builders, and social media platforms are enough to begin. Do not spend money before earning it.
  • Set clear boundaries for work time. Your family needs you present. Protect that time firmly and work around it, not through it.
  • Price your work properly. Many women undercharge because they lack confidence. Calculate your costs honestly and value your time.
  • Find a support network. Other women entrepreneurs on Telegram or WhatsApp groups can offer advice, encouragement, and honest feedback.

She also warns against a common trap: comparing your beginning to someone else's middle. "I used to feel discouraged seeing big accounts with thousands of followers. Then I realised every one of them started with zero. Focus on your own growth, one stitch at a time."

The Road Ahead: What Comes Next

Today, Noura employs two part-time assistants - a university student who handles social media and a local tailor who helps with production during peak seasons. She is developing her own fabric line and plans to launch a small collection for Ramadan 2027. Her children now help with packaging on weekends, and her eldest daughter has started learning to sew.

"I tell my daughter that this business is proof that you do not need to choose between being a mother and having your own ambition. You can build both, one day at a time."

The dining table where it all began is still there. These days it holds fabric swatches instead of schoolbooks, and the laptop stays open through dinner. The house still fills with the sounds of children, the sewing machine hums in the afternoon, and Noura works the quiet hours of the morning and night - not because she has to, but because she built something that matters to her.

Can a mother with young children really build an online business in Saudi Arabia?

Yes. Many Saudi mothers run successful online businesses from home. The key is starting small, leveraging digital tools, and building a flexible routine that fits around family needs. Platforms like Instagram and Shopify have made it easier than ever to launch without large capital.

What is the best online business for a Saudi mother to start from home?

Popular home-based online businesses include selling handmade products, offering tutoring or consulting services, freelance writing or design, dropshipping, and content creation. The best choice depends on your skills, interests, and available time.

How many hours a day does a home-based online business require?

Most successful home-based business owners start with 1 to 3 hours per day. As the business grows, the time commitment may increase. The advantage of working from home is the ability to work in small chunks around your children's schedule.

Do I need a business license to run an online business from home in Saudi Arabia?

It depends on the type and scale of the business. Freelance work may require a freelance license through the Ministry of Human Resources. Selling products commercially may require a commercial registration. It is best to check with the relevant Saudi authorities for your specific situation.

How do you balance childcare with running an online business?

Balance comes through structured routines, clear boundaries, and realistic expectations. Many mothers work during nap times, early mornings, or after bedtime. Having a dedicated workspace at home and communicating clear work hours with family members also helps maintain focus.
