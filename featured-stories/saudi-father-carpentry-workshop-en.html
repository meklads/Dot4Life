Saudi Father Turned His Garage Into a Carpentry Workshop to Teach His Sons This Summer

June 17, 2026 8 min read Featured Stories

The sound of a hand saw cuts through the silence - and for the first time since the summer break began, it drowns out the ping of notifications. In a modest garage in Riyadh, Abu Khaled stands with his three sons, each wearing safety goggles and holding a piece of wood. This scene looks nothing like any previous summer. Two years ago, Abu Khaled's boys spent most of their vacation glued to screens. Today, they're busy making something real with their hands - and they actually look happy.

This isn't just a story about woodworking. It's about how one Saudi father turned a common summer problem - boredom, screens, empty hours - into an opportunity to teach his sons life skills, strengthen their relationship, and build their confidence. The best part? Any parent can try this, even without a garage or fancy tools.

How It Started

Abu Khaled, a government employee in his forties, recalls: "Summer 2024 was the hardest. The house was full of arguments over phones. I'd come home from work and find my kids glued to their screens. I felt like I was losing them." He tried several solutions: short trips, summer camps, board games. But the fixes were temporary, and the boys always ended up back in their rooms with their devices.

One day, while fixing a broken wooden shelf in the garage, he noticed his eldest son (14) watching him with genuine curiosity. "He saw me cutting a piece of wood and asked, 'Dad, can I try?' I said, 'Sure, but carefully.' I never expected him to pick up the saw and get the motion right on his first try."

In that simple moment, an idea was born. What if he turned the garage into a small workshop where his boys could spend part of their day? No video games. No YouTube. Just wood, tools, and real creativity.

The Garage Becomes a Workshop

The start was modest. Abu Khaled didn't buy expensive power tools. He started with the basics: a hand saw, a hammer, nails, measuring tape, sandpaper, and scrap wood from a nearby hardware store. Total cost: under $80 (300 SAR). "You don't need to start big. The important thing is to start. Even a small corner in your yard or balcony will do."

He dedicated two hours every morning (9 to 11 AM) for workshop time. At first, the boys came out of curiosity. But as days passed, curiosity turned into genuine interest.

Five Unexpected Parenting Lessons (Beyond Carpentry)

Every morning in the workshop, the boys learn far more than woodworking skills:

  1. Patience & Persistence: Carpentry offers no instant gratification. It requires planning, precise measuring, careful cutting.
  2. Applied Math: Measuring with a ruler, calculating angles, estimating quantities. Math teachers noticed improvement.
  3. Confidence & Pride: When a child finishes a wooden piece and hangs it in his room, he feels genuine pride.
  4. Problem Solving: The workshop teaches kids systematic thinking: identify the problem, analyze, adjust, repeat.
  5. Time With Dad: "Now, in the workshop, we discuss, plan, work together. Nothing can replace that time."

5 Practical Tips for Any Parent to Get Started

  1. You don't need a garage or expensive tools. A small corner in the yard or a service room works.
  2. Make it a fixed time. Dedicate 1-2 hours daily at the same time.
  3. Safety first. Safety goggles, gloves, constant supervision with sharp tools.
  4. Let them learn from mistakes. Don't rush in to correct every error.
  5. Celebrate the finished product. Every piece should have a place in your home.

What age is appropriate for a child to start using hand tools?
Ages 6-7 can use simple tools under direct supervision: a small hammer, a hand saw, sandpaper. Ages 10+ can handle more precise tools. Ages 13+ can use light power tools under direct adult guidance.
