🕋 Umrah with Kids: A Complete Family Guide

From planning to return — everything a Gulf family needs for a peaceful, meaningful Umrah experience with children
📖 6 min read · Islamic

I've heard many Gulf mothers say: "Umrah with children? Too difficult — I can't concentrate, the kids cry, it's hot, and the crowds are overwhelming."

Yes, Umrah with children has its challenges. But with proper planning, it can be an unforgettable spiritual experience — for both parents and children alike. In fact, it might be the best investment in your child's love for the Holy Mosque and worship.

This guide shares practical Umrah with children tips from Gulf mothers who have done it themselves.

1. Choose the Right Time

Timing is half the success. Best times for Umrah with kids:

  • Winter season (November-February): Pleasant weather, less crowded
  • Weekdays (Sunday-Wednesday): Much less crowded than Thursday-Friday
  • Fajr or Isha time: Less crowded than other prayer times
  • Avoid public holidays: School breaks and long holidays create massive crowds
💡 Practical Tip: If your children are young (under 6), try performing Umrah late at night or just after Fajr. The weather is cool, space is available, and children often sleep in their strollers.

2. Essential Preparations

  • 🚼 Stroller: Essential — choose a lightweight, foldable one that fits through corridors
  • 💧 Water and light snacks: Biscuits, dried fruit, juice — never let a child go hungry
  • 🧴 Sunscreen and moisturizer: Even in winter, the sun in Mecca is strong
  • 📱 Tablet or quiet toy: For waiting during crowded times
  • 👕 Extra clothes: For each child — sweating and spills are inevitable
  • 🏥 First-aid kit: Fever reducer, band-aids, sanitizer
  • 📄 Umrah permits: Book permits via "Eitmarna" or "Tawakkalna" apps before traveling

3. Before Tawaf: Prepare Your Children

Psychological preparation is as important as practical preparation:

  • Tell them stories about the Holy Mosque and the Kaaba days before traveling
  • Watch short children's videos about Umrah together
  • Teach them simple supplications: "Labbayk Allahumma Umrah", "O Allah, forgive me and my parents"
  • Agree on a secret signal if they get tired or need a break
  • Promise a reward after Umrah (favorite meal, small toy)

4. During Tawaf and Sa'i

🚶 Tawaf (Circumambulation)

  • Perform tawaf on the upper levels (ground floor or first floor) where it's more spacious and less crowded
  • Don't pressure yourself to complete all seven circuits at once — take breaks between circuits
  • If a child gets tired, sit on the side of the mosque until they calm down
  • A young child in a stroller — you can push them ahead of you during tawaf
  • Remember: the reward of Umrah with children and its difficulty is greater with Allah

🏃 Sa'i (Walking between Safa and Marwah)

  • The distance is longer — make sure the stroller is comfortable
  • There are air conditioners and moving walkways — use them to reduce fatigue
  • Turn each circuit into a game: "This one is for Dad, this one for Mom, this one for you"
  • The distance between Safa and Marwah is about 400 meters — 3.5 km total for the complete Sa'i

5. Golden Tips from Mothers Who've Done It

👩‍👧 From a Saudi mother who did Umrah with 3 children: "The most important thing is not to forget your goal. You came for worship, not just to complete rituals. If you walk two circuits and the child cries and you sit for an hour calming them — that's also worship. Patience with children is worship."

👨‍👦 From an Emirati father: "Divide the tasks. I go with my older son in tawaf, my wife with the younger one in the stroller. After half the circuits, we switch. Distribution makes everyone comfortable."

6. After Your Return: Preserve the Memory

  • Hang a family photo in front of the Kaaba in a frame at home
  • Tell the grandparents about the Umrah journey — with children participating
  • Buy a special prayer rug for each child from Mecca — to maintain the connection
  • Make Umrah an annual family tradition — every year at the same time

Summary

Umrah with children isn't impossible — it's different. It requires more patience, more planning, but the reward is greater. Imagine that the young child you carried during tawaf will remember this journey for the rest of their life — and the love of the Holy Mosque will remain in their heart. Isn't that worth the effort?
