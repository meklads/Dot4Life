Lease vs Buy a Car: Which Is Better for Gulf Families?

4 min read

Ahmed Car Story: Lease-to-Own vs Buying in Saudi Arabia

"I get confused every time I think about changing my car." This is how Ahmed, a 35-year-old Saudi government employee and father of two from Riyadh, describes his dilemma between lease-to-own financing and direct purchase. Two years ago, after the birth of his second child, Ahmed needed a family car. He went to a Toyota dealership in Riyadh looking for a 2024 Camry, and the dealer offered him two options: lease-to-own financing at 1,900 SAR per month for 5 years with ownership at the end, or a cash purchase at 105,000 SAR. Ahmed did not have 105,000 SAR in cash, so he chose the lease-to-own option.

Today, two years later, Ahmed is paying 1,900 SAR every month, totaling 45,600 SAR paid so far, with 3 more years of payments remaining. His colleague Saad bought the same car in cash after saving for 3 years, and now pays nothing while enjoying his car fully. The difference is that Saad chose patience and saving, while Ahmed chose immediate comfort and installments. Ahmed story summarizes the dilemma faced by millions of Saudi and Gulf families: is lease-to-own a smart choice or an unnecessary additional cost paid over years? According to the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA), lease-to-own financing accounts for approximately 35 percent of all vehicle financing in the Kingdom, meaning hundreds of thousands of families face this decision annually. Understanding the differences between the two options can save you tens of thousands of riyals in the long run.

What Is Lease-to-Own and How Does It Work?

Lease-to-own (Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek) is a financing contract that allows you to use a car in exchange for monthly installments, with a promise to transfer ownership to you at the end of the contract term. The contract term typically ranges from 3 to 5 years. After paying all installments, the car ownership is officially transferred to your name. This type of financing differs from traditional leasing where you do not own the car at the end, and from conventional bank financing where you own the car immediately but repay a loan. In the Saudi system, lease-to-own contracts are regulated by the Saudi Central Bank, which has established rules protecting both parties. The main advantage of this type is that monthly installments are lower than traditional car financing for the same term, because the financing only covers the difference between the new car price and its expected value at the end of the contract. However, the main disadvantage is that you do not own the car until you pay all installments, and if you default on payments, you may lose both the car and the installments you have paid.

Direct Purchase: Cash or Bank Financing

Direct car purchase, either in cash or through conventional bank financing, remains the preferred option for those who have liquidity or can obtain bank financing. In a cash purchase, you pay the full car price at once and become the owner immediately. The major advantage is that you pay no interest or additional profits, and there are no future monthly commitments. The disadvantage is that you need a large amount of cash that may consume your savings. In conventional bank financing, the bank pays the full car price to the dealership, and you repay the loan in monthly installments with a profit margin. The advantage is that you own the car immediately and repay over a long period, while the disadvantage is that monthly installments are higher than lease-to-own because you are financing the full car value, not just the difference. In Saudi Arabia, banks offer car financing up to 70 to 90 percent of the car value, with repayment periods up to 5 years, and profit rates ranging from 2.5 to 5 percent depending on the bank and the borrower credit score.

How to Choose: 5 Critical Factors

Choosing the right option depends on your personal situation, not on what is common among friends or relatives. Here are 7 factors you should weigh before making a decision. First, how long you keep the car: if you change cars every 3 to 4 years, lease-to-own may be practical. If you keep a car for 5 years or more, direct purchase is more economical. Second, your down payment: if you do not have 20 to 30 percent of the car value in cash, lease-to-own offers easier entry. Third, your monthly budget: if you prefer lower monthly payments, lease-to-own offers lower installments. If you can afford slightly higher payments for immediate ownership, bank financing is better. Fourth, the importance of immediate ownership: if you need the car as collateral for other financing or want complete freedom to sell or modify, purchase is necessary. Fifth, your credit score: a good credit score gives you better financing options in both systems. Sixth, maintenance and operating costs: in lease-to-own, maintenance and insurance are often included in the contract, reducing monthly surprises. In direct purchase, these costs are your responsibility and may reach 3,000 to 5,000 SAR annually for maintenance and 1,500 to 3,000 SAR for insurance. Seventh, future car value: cars lose 15 to 25 percent of their value in the first year and 40 to 60 percent after 5 years. In lease-to-own, you do not bear the risk of value depreciation because you own at the end at a pre-agreed price. Negotiation Tips for the Best Car Deal in Saudi Arabia

Whether you choose lease-to-own or direct purchase, good negotiation can save you thousands of riyals. Start by researching car prices online before visiting showrooms, and use price comparison sites. Do not accept the first price offer, negotiate with multiple dealers and tell each one you are shopping for the best deal. In lease-to-own, negotiate the car price itself, not just the monthly payment, because a lower car price reduces every installment throughout the contract term. The profit margin is negotiable with the bank or finance company, especially if you have a good credit score and a clean credit history. A 1 percent reduction in the profit margin could save you 2,000 to 4,000 SAR over 5 years. Understand all additional fees before signing: administration fees that may reach 2,500 SAR, vehicle registration fees, comprehensive insurance that may be mandatory, and early or late payment penalties that may reach 5 percent of the remaining amount. Read the entire contract before signing, do not hesitate to ask for clarification on any unclear clause, and take the contract home to review with a lawyer or financial advisor if needed. Ask about the possibility of early repayment without penalties, as this gives you additional flexibility if your financial situation improves. The best time to buy a car is at the end of the year or month when dealerships need to meet sales targets and are willing to offer real discounts. When a new model is released, prices of the old model drop by 10 to 20 percent, potentially saving you thousands on a similar car.

Common Mistakes Car Buyers Make in Saudi Arabia

Many Gulf families make costly mistakes when buying or leasing a car. First, focusing only on the monthly payment rather than the total cost. A low monthly payment over 5 years may mean a much higher total cost. Second, buying a car beyond their means because "friends drive luxury cars." The 20/4/10 rule is the smart benchmark: 20 percent down payment, 4-year financing term, and monthly payment not exceeding 10 percent of your monthly income. Third, neglecting operating costs: fuel, regular maintenance, insurance, tires, all add up to 800 to 1,500 SAR in additional monthly costs. Fourth, not having a used car inspected by a specialist technician before purchase, which could cost thousands in unexpected repairs. Fifth, signing a financing contract without reading the fine print that may contain hidden penalties and fees. Frequently Asked Questions About Car Financing in Saudi Arabia

Is lease-to-own financing Sharia-compliant?

Lease-to-own financing in Saudi Arabia is regulated by the Senior Scholars Council and the Capital Market Authority. The approved form is an Ijara (lease) contract with a promise of ownership at the end, which is Sharia-compliant provided that the car remains the property of the bank or finance company throughout the contract period, and the promise of ownership is separate from the lease contract. Ensure the contract you sign is Sharia-compliant.

Can I sell the car during a lease-to-own contract?

In most lease-to-own contracts, you cannot sell the car because you are not the owner until all installments are paid. Some contracts allow early termination with a penalty fee. If you want to sell, you may need to pay the full remaining amount first to become the owner, then sell. Check your contract terms carefully.

What is the difference between lease-to-own and bank financing?

In lease-to-own, the bank owns the car and you pay rent until the term ends and ownership transfers to you. The monthly payment is lower because you finance only the value difference. In conventional bank financing, you own the car immediately and repay a loan. The payment is higher but you own from day one and can sell or modify the car freely.

What is the best term length for lease-to-own?

The most common term is 5 years, which balances reasonable monthly payments with acceptable total cost. A shorter term of 3 years gives higher payments but lower total cost. A longer term of 5 to 7 years gives lower payments but significantly higher total cost. Choose the term that fits your budget without exceeding 5 years if possible.

Can expats buy a car on installments in Saudi Arabia?

Yes, expats can buy cars on installments in Saudi Arabia, but the conditions are stricter than for citizens. Banks typically require a higher down payment of up to 40 percent, a shorter financing period of 3 years maximum, and a higher profit rate. A valid residency permit (Iqama), steady salary, and a Saudi bank account are also required. Some banks require salary transfer to them as a condition for financing.
