Pregnancy Week by Week Calculator 2025: A Clear Guide for Saudi Arabia and the GCC

🏥 Health📅 2026-06-21

Two pink lines on a test, and suddenly your first question is the same one every mother asks: how far along am I, and when is the baby due? A pregnancy week by week calculator answers exactly that. Here is the short answer: it counts your pregnancy from the first day of your last menstrual period, estimates which week and trimester you are in, and projects a due date roughly 40 weeks, or about 280 days, from that first day (Johns Hopkins Medicine). It gives you a timeline to follow, not a fixed promise, and it helps you know what to expect at each stage.

This guide explains how the calculator works, what happens across the three trimesters, and why your due date is an estimate your doctor may adjust, with practical notes for expectant mothers in Saudi Arabia and the GCC.

How the Calculator Works

The most common method counts pregnancy from the first day of your last menstrual period, known as the LMP. That day is treated as day one, even though conception usually happens about two weeks later. From there, a full-term pregnancy averages 280 days, or 40 weeks (Johns Hopkins Medicine).

To estimate the due date, many clinicians use a simple method called Naegele's rule: take the first day of your LMP, add seven days, then subtract three months. This assumes a regular 28-day cycle, so if your cycles are longer or shorter, the date may need adjusting (Calculator Academy). Our calculator handles this arithmetic for you. The Three Trimesters at a Glance

Pregnancy is divided into three trimesters, each with its own rhythm. The table below maps them out:

What Happens in the First Trimester

The first trimester covers weeks 0 to 12, and it is a period of rapid, invisible change. The fertilised egg implants, and the foundations of the baby's organs begin to form. For many mothers, this is also the most physically demanding stretch, with fatigue, nausea, and tender breasts being common.

This is the stage to confirm your pregnancy with a healthcare provider and begin prenatal care. A first-trimester ultrasound, done before 13 weeks, gives the most accurate dating of your pregnancy, which is why your due date from the calculator may be refined after that scan (Johns Hopkins Medicine). Folic acid and early check-ups matter most here.

What Happens in the Second Trimester

Weeks 13 to 26 are often described as the most comfortable stretch. The nausea of the early weeks usually eases, energy tends to return, and the bump becomes visible. Many mothers feel the baby's first movements during this time, a milestone that makes the pregnancy feel suddenly real.

This trimester usually includes important scans and screenings, including the anatomy ultrasound that checks the baby's development. It is a good window to plan practical matters calmly, from maternity arrangements to preparing the home, before the heavier final months arrive.

What Happens in the Third Trimester

The final stretch, weeks 27 to 40, is about growth and preparation. The baby gains weight steadily, and your body works harder, which often brings back fatigue along with back discomfort and trouble sleeping. Check-ups become more frequent as your due date approaches.

This is the time to prepare for birth in earnest: your hospital bag, your birth plan, and the questions you want to ask your care team. Remember that a due date is an estimate. Many babies arrive in the weeks around it rather than exactly on it, so treat the date as a guide, not a deadline.

Why Your Due Date Is an Estimate

The single most useful thing to understand is that your due date is a projection, not a guarantee. The LMP method assumes a regular cycle, and not every cycle is regular. That is why an early ultrasound, which measures the baby directly, often gives a more accurate date and may shift the estimate by a few days (Johns Hopkins Medicine).

So if your calculator date and your doctor's date differ slightly, that is normal and expected. Your care provider's dating, especially when based on an early scan, takes precedence. The calculator is a helpful starting point for planning, while your medical team gives you the dating to rely on.

Using the Calculator in the GCC

For expectant mothers in Saudi Arabia and the wider GCC, a week-by-week calculator is a simple way to stay oriented between appointments and to know what to ask at each visit. It is especially handy for keeping track of which trimester you are in and roughly when key scans fall.

It does not replace your antenatal care. Local clinics and hospitals follow established schedules for check-ups and screenings, and your doctor remains your primary guide. Think of the calculator as a companion that helps you feel informed and prepared, not as a substitute for professional care.

FAQ

How is my due date calculated?

Most commonly from the first day of your last menstrual period, adding about 40 weeks. Naegele's rule adds seven days and subtracts three months from that first day, assuming a regular 28-day cycle.

Why does my due date change after an ultrasound?

An early ultrasound measures the baby directly and often dates the pregnancy more accurately than the LMP method, so your provider may adjust the estimate by a few days.

How many weeks is a full pregnancy?

A full-term pregnancy averages about 40 weeks, or 280 days, from the first day of the last menstrual period, divided into three trimesters.

Is the calculator accurate if my cycle is irregular?

The LMP method assumes a regular cycle, so an irregular cycle can make the estimate less precise. An early ultrasound gives more reliable dating in that case.

Does a due date mean the baby arrives that exact day?

No. The due date is an estimate, and many babies arrive in the weeks around it. Treat it as a guide and follow your care team's advice as the time approaches.
