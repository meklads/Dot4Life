# Jeddah Mortgage Calculator 2025: What Your Monthly Payment Really Costs

Before you sign a home finance contract in Jeddah, ask yourself one question: do you know what this house costs you over twenty years, not just what this month's payment is? Here is the direct answer. Your monthly payment is set by exactly three numbers, the financed amount after your down payment, the annual profit rate, and the repayment term. Yet the figure that surprises buyers later is not the payment itself, it is the total they pay on top of the home's price. In this guide we show you how to calculate the payment yourself, how much down payment Jeddah requires today, and where the costs most buyers forget tend to hide.

Home finance is not a decision you reverse easily. It is a commitment that can run thirty years. So it deserves half an hour with the numbers before you sit across from a bank officer. Let us start with the basics.

## How Is the Monthly Mortgage Payment Calculated?

The monthly payment is not a number the bank picks at random. It is the result of a fixed formula built on three inputs: the financed amount, the profit rate, and the number of months. The idea is simple. The bank spreads the amount plus its profit across the full term in equal installments, so the balance lands at exactly zero in the final month.

What matters to you in practice is the effect of each input. The longer the term, the smaller the monthly payment but the larger the total profit you pay. The reverse holds too: a shorter term means a heavier payment but a lighter total cost. The profit rate is the most sensitive lever of all, because a small difference in it produces thousands of riyals across the years.

This is why you should never judge by the payment alone when comparing two offers. An offer with a lower payment but a longer term can cost you far more in the end. The golden rule: always compare by the total amount paid over the full term, not by the monthly payment, because the payment can mislead while the total tells the truth.

## How Much Down Payment Do You Need in Jeddah?

This is the question that stops most buyers. The down payment in Saudi Arabia is governed by the loan-to-value ratio, known as LTV. The Saudi Central Bank raised the maximum LTV to 90% for a citizen's first home, which means the minimum down payment for a qualifying first home can drop to around 10% of the property value ([SAMA Rulebook](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/increase-maximum-ltv-real-estate-finance-granted-banks-and-exchangers-citizens-own-first-home)).

But the first home is a special case. For a second home and beyond the ratio stays lower, so you need a larger down payment. Non-citizens typically face tougher terms and higher deposits as well. On top of that, the regulatory minimum is one thing, and what your bank actually asks based on your credit profile is another that may be higher.

Here is the key point: do not budget the down payment alone. There are one-time transaction costs paid on top of it, usually including valuation, documentation, and sometimes an agent commission. Set aside a reserve for them so the closing day does not catch you off guard. And most important, do not empty your emergency savings into the down payment. A house with no liquidity cushion leaves you exposed to debt at the first breakdown or setback.

## What Does the Profit Rate Mean and How Do You Compare Offers?

The profit rate is the cost of the money you borrowed, and it is the number that most deserves scrutiny. Offers usually come in two flavours: a fixed rate for the whole term, or a variable rate tied to a market index that rises and falls. Fixed gives you the certainty of an unchanging payment. Variable can be cheaper at first but carries the risk of climbing later. Variable finance rates broadly track the Saudi Central Bank's policy rate, which the bank cut to 4.50% in October 2025 ([Saudi Central Bank](https://www.sama.gov.sa/en-us/mediacenter/news/pages/news-1115.aspx)), and that benchmark feeds through over time into the cost of finance offered to you.

When comparing, do not stop at the headline rate. Ask for the annual percentage figure that bundles profit with fees, because it shows the true cost of the finance. An offer with a lower profit rate but higher fees can in reality be pricier than one with a higher rate and lower fees.

Ask each bank for the full repayment schedule in writing, and look at two columns: the monthly payment and the total paid. Those two columns settle the comparison honestly. And do not hesitate to negotiate. Some terms are open to discussion, especially if your credit profile is strong.

## Common Mistakes Jeddah Buyers Make

The first mistake is fixating on the monthly payment and ignoring the total. A buyer celebrates a low payment without noticing they added ten extra years of repayment and paid heavy profit for them. A comfortable payment is not automatically the cheaper deal.

The second mistake is draining all savings into the down payment to shrink the installment. The result is a home with no safety net, so the first health or job emergency pushes you into borrowing at a higher rate. Always keep an emergency fund that covers several months of expenses.

The third mistake is forgetting post-purchase costs: routine maintenance, compound service fees, and sometimes insurance. These are ongoing items added to the payment and they never appear in the bank's calculator. Budget for them upfront so your numbers stay realistic. The fourth mistake is signing without reading the early-settlement clause and any fees tied to it.

## How to Prepare Before Visiting the Bank

Good preparation saves you time and money. Start by knowing your real buying power: how large a monthly payment can you carry without choking your budget? The cautious rule is to keep the payment from swallowing so much of your income that nothing is left for living costs and saving.

Gather your paperwork early: proof of income, account statements, and your existing obligations. The clearer your file, the stronger your negotiation. Request offers from more than one bank, because the gap between offers can be large across the years. Do not settle for the bank that simply receives your salary.

Finally, calculate before you visit. Open the tool, run the numbers, and learn your expected payment range before an officer tells you. When you arrive armed with figures, you negotiate from strength rather than waiting to be convinced.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the minimum down payment for a home in Jeddah?**
For a citizen's first home, the central bank raised the maximum LTV to 90%, meaning a minimum down payment of roughly 10% of the property value in qualifying cases. For second homes and non-citizens the ratio is lower and the deposit higher. Confirm your bank's actual requirement.

**Is a shorter or longer term better?**
A longer term shrinks the monthly payment but grows the total profit you pay. A shorter term does the opposite. Choose a term that keeps your payment comfortable without stretching it needlessly. Balance payment comfort against lifetime cost.

**What is the difference between fixed and variable rates?**
Fixed stays the same for the whole term, so your payment is stable. Variable is tied to a market index and moves up and down, and may be cheaper at first but carries upside risk. Choose based on your ability to absorb a future change in the payment.

**Does the calculator include every cost?**
A payment calculator computes the finance and its profit only. Transaction costs, maintenance, service fees, and insurance sit outside it. Add these items manually to know the full real cost, not the bank installment alone.

**Can I settle early?**
Usually yes, but conditions or fees may apply depending on the contract and regulations. Read the early-settlement clause carefully before signing, and ask the bank directly about any cost tied to it before you plan to accelerate.

## Practical Takeaway

Your monthly payment is set by three numbers: the financed amount, the profit rate, and the term. But the smart decision does not stop at the payment, it compares the total paid over the full term. Know your required down payment, calculate your realistic buying power, keep an emergency fund, and compare offers from more than one bank by the annual percentage figure rather than the headline rate alone. An hour of calculation saves you years of regret.
