First-Time Home Buyer Guide Saudi Arabia: Your Step-by-Step Path to Ownership

According to REGA, Buying your first home in Saudi Arabia is a milestone every Gulf family dreams of. Whether you are a first home buyer in Saudi or a Gulf family seeking real estate in Saudi Arabia, the path to buy a house in Saudi can feel complex. For years you have watched the rent cheques pile up, seen friends take possession of their villas, and wondered when your turn would come. The good news is that 2026 is one of the best years in recent memory for first-time buyers in the Kingdom. Government programmes, falling real estate entry thresholds, and a wave of new developments across Riyadh, Jeddah, and the Eastern Province have opened doors that were firmly shut a decade ago.

Yet the process can feel overwhelming. Between the Sakani application, the REDF loan, the Saudi mortgage pre-approval, the endless property viewings, and the paper-thin line between a good deal and a costly mistake, a first buyer needs a clear map. This guide is that map. It walks you through every step, from assessing your budget to signing the final contract, with real numbers, practical checklists, and honest advice for Gulf families taking their first step onto the property ladder.

Why Now Is the Time to Buy Your First Home in Saudi Arabia

The Saudi real estate market is undergoing a transformation driven by Vision 2030. The government has committed to increasing home ownership among Saudi citizens to 70% and beyond, and the numbers show real progress. According to the Ministry of Municipal and Rural Affairs and Housing, home ownership rates have already climbed past 67% as of early 2026 (Ministry of Housing).

Three forces are working in your favour right now:

  1. Interest rates are stable. After the global rate hikes of 2022, 2024, the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) has maintained a steadier environment. REDF-subsidised mortgages remain among the most affordable in the region, with profit rates starting as low as 2.5% for eligible Sakani beneficiaries.
  2. New supply is entering the market. Mega-developments in northern Riyadh, the Jeddah waterfront, and the Dammam metropolitan area mean buyers have more options than ever. This increased supply puts gentle pressure on prices and gives you room to negotiate.
  3. Government incentives are at their peak. The Sakani programme, administered by the Ministry of Housing, offers free land allocations, ready-built units at subsidised prices, and reduced mortgage financing through the Real Estate Development Fund (REDF). The Wafi programme extends these benefits to specific family segments.

Key Insight: The average waiting period for Sakani-approved beneficiaries has dropped to under 18 months in many cities, down from over 3 years a decade ago. If you are a Saudi citizen earning under SAR 18,000 monthly, the window of opportunity has never been wider.

Step 1: Know Your Budget , What Can You Really Afford?

Before you scroll through property listings, you need a number. Not a rough idea , an exact figure of what you can spend. Lenders in Saudi Arabia typically allow a monthly instalment of no more than 30% to 35% of your gross monthly income. Some stretch to 40% for high-income earners, but the conservative 30% line is safer for family finances.

Do not forget to factor in your existing obligations. If you already have a car loan or personal finance instalment, the bank will deduct that from your available capacity. A clean credit record through the Saudi Credit Bureau (SIMAH) improves your negotiating position significantly.

Step 2: Choose the Right City and Neighbourhood

Saudi Arabia is a vast country, and each city offers a different value proposition for first-time buyers. Here is a breakdown of the major centres:

Riyadh

The capital offers the strongest long-term appreciation potential. Neighbourhoods such as Al Nuzha, Al Malqa, and Al Yasmin are popular with families. A 3-bedroom apartment in a good compound costs SAR 700,000, 1,200,000 while a small villa in the northern suburbs starts around SAR 1,200,000. Riyadh's population continues to surge, which supports rental demand and property values. The main challenge for first-time buyers is that desirable areas close to the city centre are expensive, while cheaper land on the outskirts can mean long commutes.

Jeddah

Jeddah offers more affordable entry points than Riyadh, particularly in neighbourhoods such as Al Salamah, Al Rowdah, and Al Zahra. A 3-bedroom apartment can be found for SAR 450,000, 800,000, and a villa in a developing area such as Obhur or north Jeddah starts around SAR 900,000. The Red Sea coast adds lifestyle appeal, and the growing tourism sector under Vision 2030 is boosting the local economy. Buyers should watch for flooding risk in low-lying areas and factor in maintenance for older buildings in humid conditions.

Dammam / Khobar / Dhahran (Eastern Province)

The Eastern Province metropolitan area offers some of the best value in the Kingdom. A 3-bedroom apartment in Khobar's Al Dhahran or Al Qusour neighbourhood costs SAR 400,000, 700,000, and villas in Dammam's Al Faisaliyah or Al Shati areas start around SAR 800,000. The presence of Saudi Aramco stabilises the economy and supports property demand. The quieter pace of life and shorter commutes appeal to families who work in the energy sector or nearby industrial cities.

Makkah and Madinah

These holy cities have a unique property market driven by religious tourism and the Umrah sector. First-time buyers who are Saudi citizens and work in these cities can benefit from dedicated Sakani allocations. Property prices range widely, but a 2-bedroom apartment in a satellite district of Makkah can cost SAR 350,000, 600,000. The rental yield potential is strong due to the constant flow of pilgrims, making these cities attractive for those who may later rent out their property.

Step 3: The Sakani Program and Government Support

If you are a Saudi citizen earning less than SAR 18,000 per month, the Sakani program is your most powerful tool. Launched by the Ministry of Housing, Sakani provides three main pathways to home ownership:

  1. Free Land Allocation: Eligible citizens receive a plot of land free of charge, typically in a new or developing suburb. The beneficiary must build within a specified period, usually 3 to 5 years, often with the help of an approved contractor.
  2. Ready-Built Units: Sakani offers fully constructed villas or apartments at subsidised prices. Prices are negotiated with developers and are significantly below market rates. Payment plans are spread over the construction period and often require no down payment.
  3. Subsidised Mortgage Financing (REDF): The Real Estate Development Fund provides subsidised profit rates as low as 2.5% for eligible beneficiaries. This reduces the monthly instalment by 30% to 50% compared with a standard bank mortgage.

To apply, visit sakani.sa and create an account. You will need your national ID, proof of income, and family status documents. The system will calculate your eligibility score, which is based on income, family size, age, and whether you have previously owned a home. Once approved, you can select your preferred pathway and begin the process.

Tip: Even if you think you might not qualify, apply anyway. The eligibility criteria have been expanded several times since the programme launched in 2017. Many families earning up to SAR 18,000 who thought they were excluded were found eligible after the 2024 revisions.

Step 4: Mortgage Pre-Approval , Your Buying Power in Writing

Mortgage pre-approval is the single most important document you will obtain before shopping for a home. It tells you exactly how much a bank is willing to lend you, and it signals to sellers and real estate agents that you are a serious buyer.

To get pre-approved, visit at least three banks , Al Rajhi Bank, Al Ahli Bank (NCB), and Alinma Bank are the three largest mortgage providers in Saudi Arabia. Each will ask for:

  • Copy of your national ID (or iqama for non-Saudi Gulf families)
  • Proof of income: salary certificate from your employer or bank statements showing regular deposits
  • Credit report from SIMAH: you can request yours at simah.sa
  • Details of any existing debts or financial obligations
  • Employment contract or letter confirming tenure (most banks require at least 6 months with the same employer)

Most banks respond within 3 to 7 working days. The pre-approval is valid for 30 to 90 days, so time your search to match. Compare the offers carefully: look not only at the profit rate but also at the administrative fees, early settlement penalties, and whether the rate is fixed or variable. A rate that is 0.5% lower but comes with a high early settlement fee may cost you more if you plan to move within 10 years.

Step 5: Finding the Right Property

With your budget clear and pre-approval in hand, the search begins. In Saudi Arabia, the property market operates through a mix of real estate agents (known as "simsar" or "al samasira"), online portals such as Aqar (aqar.com), and direct owner listings. Key steps:

  1. Define your must-haves. Is a separate maids room essential? Do you need a ground-floor apartment for elderly parents? How close must you be to schools and mosques? Write these down before you view a single property.
  2. Use the Aqar platform. Aqar.com is the most widely used property portal in Saudi. Filter by city, neighbourhood, property type, price range, and size. Save searches and set alerts for new listings.
  3. View at least 8 to 10 properties. Most first-time buyers fall in love with the first or second property they see. Resist the urge. Viewing a wide range gives you perspective on what SAR 800,000 actually gets you in different neighbourhoods.
  4. Check the title deed (Sak or "صك"). Ensure the property has a clear title deed registered with the Ministry of Justice. Your real estate agent should provide this. If the property is still under construction (off-plan), verify that the developer is licensed by the Ministry of Housing.
  5. Inspect thoroughly. Check for water damage, electrical quality, plumbing condition, and structural issues. In Jeddah, pay special attention to drainage and humidity. In Riyadh, check the air conditioning system , a central AC replacement can cost SAR 30,000.

Step 6: The Purchase Process , From Offer to Ownership

Once you have found the right property and agreed on a price with the seller, the process moves through several legal and financial stages:

  1. Sign the preliminary sale agreement (Mubaya'ah or "مبايعة"). This document records the agreed price, payment schedule, and handover date. It is legally binding once signed by both parties and witnessed. You pay a deposit, typically 5% to 10% of the purchase price, which is credited toward the total.
  2. Finalise the mortgage with the bank. The bank will conduct an independent property valuation to confirm the property is worth the agreed price. If the valuation is lower than the sale price, you must cover the difference from your own funds. The bank then issues the final loan approval.
  3. Transfer the title deed. Both parties go to the Ministry of Justice office or an authorised notary to register the transfer of ownership. The buyer pays the transfer fee, which is 2.5% of the property value plus a small administrative fee.
  4. Pay all dues and take possession. After the title deed is registered in your name, you pay the seller the remaining amount (including the bank disbursement), and the keys are handed over. Utility connections are transferred to your name.

Important: The entire process from signed offer to handover typically takes 4 to 8 weeks. Plan your rental notice period accordingly so you do not end up paying double , rent on your old place and the mortgage on your new home.

At the standard market rate (3.5%), the monthly mortgage of SAR 6,300 in Jeddah is SAR 1,700 more than the monthly rent of SAR 4,583 (55,000 ÷ 12). But with REDF subsidised financing at 2.5%, the mortgage drops to SAR 4,200 , actually cheaper than rent. And unlike rent, a portion of each mortgage payment builds equity in your asset.

Over 10 years, renting in Riyadh costs you SAR 750,000 with nothing to show for it. Buying at the REDF rate costs you roughly SAR 504,000 in payments, of which about SAR 200,000 goes toward the principal , meaning you have built SAR 200,000 in equity. The arithmetic is clear: for families planning a long stay, buying with government support is the better financial decision.

Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

Every real estate agent and banker in Saudi Arabia has seen the same pattern of mistakes repeat with new buyers. Avoid these five:

  1. Buying without pre-approval. You find the perfect home, make an offer, and then discover the bank will only lend 70% of the price instead of 80%. Suddenly you need an extra SAR 80,000 in cash you do not have. Pre-approval prevents this.
  2. Maxing out the loan. Just because the bank approves SAR 1,200,000 does not mean you can afford the instalments comfortably. A sudden job loss, medical emergency, or car breakdown can tip the balance. Always keep your instalment within 25%, 30% of your income, not the bank's maximum.
  3. Skipping the professional inspection. In Saudi Arabia, many home sales happen "as-is." A professional inspection before signing reveals hidden problems , poor construction, sewage issues, electrical faults. SAR 2,000 on an inspection can save SAR 50,000 in repairs.
  4. Ignoring the neighbourhood's future development. A cheap property on the edge of the city may seem like a bargain, but if the area has no schools, no mosques, and no planned metro extension, resale will be difficult. Research the municipal development plan for the area before buying.
  5. Overlooking the service charge and maintenance in apartment buildings. In shared buildings, the annual service charge (for cleaning, security, maintenance of common areas) can be SAR 5,000, 15,000. Ask for the last 3 years of service charge statements and budget for annual increases.

FAQ , First-Time Home Buyer Saudi Arabia

What is the minimum salary for a first-time home buyer in Saudi Arabia?

Most Saudi banks require a minimum monthly income of SAR 5,000, 10,000 for mortgage eligibility. The Sakani programme helps families earning as low as SAR 4,000 to access subsidised housing, though the property options at that level are more limited.

Can expats or non-Saudi Gulf citizens buy a home in Saudi Arabia?

Yes, under certain conditions. Non-Saudi residents can purchase property for personal use in designated investment zones in Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam, and Makkah, subject to approval from the Ministry of Interior and the Ministry of Municipal and Rural Affairs. A minimum property value and specific residency status requirements apply.

What is the Sakani programme and how does it help?

Sakani is the Saudi government's flagship housing programme, offering three pathways: free land allocation, subsidised ready-built units, and reduced mortgage financing through REDF. It targets Saudi citizens earning under SAR 18,000 per month to make first home ownership financially achievable.

How much is the down payment for a first home in Saudi Arabia?

Typically 15% to 20% of the property value for a conventional mortgage. Through Sakani and REDF, the effective down payment can drop to 5%, 10%. On a SAR 800,000 home, that means SAR 40,000, 80,000 as your own cash contribution. Add another 5%, 7% for transaction costs.

Is buying better than renting in Saudi Arabia?

For families planning to stay in one city for 5 years or more, buying is almost always more cost-effective, especially with Sakani or REDF support. In Riyadh, annual rent for a family home averages SAR 60,000, 90,000. A comparable mortgage after REDF subsidy can cost less per month. Over a decade, the buyer builds substantial equity while the renter recovers nothing.
