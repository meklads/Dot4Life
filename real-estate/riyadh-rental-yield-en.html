Riyadh Rental Yield Calculator 2025: Is Your Property Actually a Smart Investment?

You bought an apartment in Riyadh to rent out, but do you know what it earns you each year as a percentage, not just in riyals? That difference is what separates a deliberate investor from someone who merely owns property. Here is the direct answer. Rental yield is the annual rent divided by the purchase price, multiplied by a hundred, giving you a percentage you can compare against any other opportunity. But the number that fools many people is the gross yield, which ignores expenses. The real return that lands in your pocket is the net yield, and it is always lower. In this guide we explain the difference, work an example in riyals, and show how Riyadh's rent freeze changed the rules of the game.

Buying property to invest ties up a large sum for years, and it can be hard to exit quickly if it underperforms. So the return deserves a careful calculation before you pay, not after you discover your numbers were optimistic. Let us begin.

What Is Rental Yield and Why Does It Matter?

Rental yield is the percentage your property earns each year relative to its price. Its value is that it turns property from an expensive asset into a number you can easily compare against other options: a deposit, a fund, or a second property in a different district. Without this percentage, you are left saying "I bought for a million and rent for sixty thousand" with no way of knowing whether that is a good deal.

The percentage alone reveals a lot. An expensive property in an upscale district may deliver a low yield because its price rose faster than its rent. A cheaper property in a growing area may deliver a higher one. The percentage puts everything on a single scale, so you decide by numbers rather than impressions.

But be careful: yield is not the whole story. A property with a high yield in a declining location may lose value over time, so the price loss eats the rental gain. Always weigh rental yield against the property's own potential for capital growth. A very high yield is sometimes a signal of higher risk, not a perfect deal.

The Difference Between Gross and Net Yield

This is where the biggest misunderstanding lives. Gross yield divides the full annual rent by the purchase price, deducting no expenses. It looks like a pretty number, but it is optimistic because it assumes you collect every riyal of rent and spend zero on the property.

Net yield is the reality. It subtracts maintenance, management fees, vacancy gaps between one tenant and the next, and insurance where it applies, then divides what remains by the purchase price. This is the figure that actually reaches your account, and it is always lower than the gross yield.

The gap between the two is not small. Market estimates suggest the difference between gross and net yield in Riyadh runs around one to one and a half percentage points, driven by vacancy, maintenance, and management fees (Sands of Wealth). The practical meaning: when someone sells you "a 9% yield," ask whether it is gross or net, because the difference can be the difference in your decision.

Why does the gap widen sometimes? Because expenses do not arrive on schedule. A single month of vacancy between tenants carves a tangible slice out of the year's rent. A major fault in the air conditioning or plumbing can swallow several months of profit. And management fees, if you hire someone to run the property for you, take a fixed cut of every rent payment. Each of these items looks small alone, but together they create the difference between an optimistic figure on paper and a real return in your account. This is why a seasoned investor calculates the net first, then decides.

A Worked Example in Riyals: Calculating an Apartment's Yield in Riyadh

Let us turn theory into numbers. Picture an apartment in Riyadh you bought for SAR 800,000, renting it for SAR 56,000 a year. The figures are illustrative for explanation only:

ItemIllustrative value
Purchase priceSAR 800,000
Annual rentSAR 56,000
Gross yield56,000 ÷ 800,000 × 100 = 7%
Estimated annual costsMaintenance + management + vacancy
Net yieldBelow gross after deducting costs

The gross yield here is 7%, a number that looks good. But deduct a month of vacancy, plus maintenance and management, and the net can clearly drop below it. That difference is what you should build your decision on, not the shiny gross figure.

Try rerunning the math with your own numbers: if the purchase price rises while rent stays flat, the yield falls. If your costs grow, the net falls further. That sensitivity is exactly what the calculator reveals, so you learn whether the property is genuinely worth it or whether the shiny number is just a gross mirage.

What Is the Average Rental Yield in Riyadh Today?

Yields in Riyadh vary by unit type and location. Market data suggests the average gross yield for residential property in Riyadh sits around the mid-five-percent range, while the average net yield runs near 4% after deducting real-world costs (Sands of Wealth).

But the average hides wide variation. Smaller units like studios and one-bedrooms tend toward higher yields because their rent is high relative to their price, while larger units in upscale districts may deliver lower yields despite their higher price. Location and unit type drive the difference more than the headline average.

So do not invest based on the "city average." Calculate the yield of the specific unit in the specific district with its real figures. The Riyadh average is a general indicator of direction, but your decision rests on your property, its rent, its price, and its real costs, not on a headline number.

Which Unit Type Delivers a Better Yield in Riyadh?

Unit type makes a large difference to yield, larger than many new investors assume. The general rule is that smaller units tend toward higher gross yields, because their rent is high relative to their lower purchase price. Studios and one-bedroom apartments often top the yield tables for this reason, especially in districts near work hubs frequented by singles and expatriates.

But the higher yield does not come free. Smaller units typically see faster tenant turnover, which means more frequent vacancy and repeated setup costs between one tenant and the next. Larger units, despite their usually lower yield, may attract families who stay for years, lowering vacancy and steadying your costs. Part of the studio's shiny number can erode in the cost of frequent turnover.

The practical takeaway is to choose the unit type by your strategy, not by the advertised yield alone. If you want higher income and can handle more active management, smaller units are a logical choice. If you prefer stability and quieter management, larger units may suit you despite their lower yield. There is no absolutely "best" type, only the type that fits your plan, your time, and your capacity to manage.

How Does the Rent Freeze Affect Your Investment?

A new factor must enter every calculation today. In September 2025, a decree fixed residential and commercial rents within Riyadh's urban area for five years, so rent may not be raised on existing contracts during that period (Arab News).

The effect on an investor is twofold. On one hand, it gives you stability and a predictable income for five years. On the other, it limits your ability to raise rent to keep pace with inflation or rising prices, so your real yield erodes gradually if your costs climb. This means the precision of your yield calculation at purchase now matters more, because you may not be able to offset a low figure by raising rent later.

The practical point: study the current regulatory framework before buying, and calculate your yield assuming a fixed rather than escalating rent during the freeze period. A decision built on the assumption of annual rent increases has become riskier in this new framework. Always verify the latest regulatory updates from official sources.

FAQ

How do I calculate rental yield quickly?

Divide the annual rent by the purchase price and multiply by a hundred, giving the gross yield. For example, 56,000 over 800,000 times a hundred equals 7%. For net yield, subtract annual expenses from the rent first, then divide. The net is what actually reaches your pocket.

What is a good yield in Riyadh?

There is no single number that fits everyone, since it depends on unit type, location, and risk. The residential market average sits around the mid-five-percent range gross and near 4% net. Compare your unit's yield against this range and against alternatives before judging.

Why is my net yield lower than expected?

Usually because of vacancy between tenants, maintenance, management fees, and insurance. These items eat into the rent before it reaches you. Budget for them upfront so your real yield is not a disappointing surprise a year after buying.

Is a higher yield always better?

Not necessarily. A very high yield may signal a higher-risk location or a chance of price decline. Weigh rental yield against capital growth and location stability. A good deal balances income and safety rather than chasing the highest number alone.

Does the rent freeze affect my property?

If your property is within Riyadh's urban area, the decree fixes rent for five years, limiting increases during that period. Calculate your yield on a fixed-rent basis, and verify the scope and latest updates from official sources before buying.

Practical Takeaway

Rental yield turns your property from a vague number into a percentage you can compare with any opportunity. But do not be fooled by the shiny gross yield, the net after expenses is what reaches your pocket, and it is always lower. Calculate the yield of your specific unit rather than the city average, study how the rent freeze affects your future income, and balance yield against capital growth and location stability. Smart investing starts with an honest calculation before you pay.
