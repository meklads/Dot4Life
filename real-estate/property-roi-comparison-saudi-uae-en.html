Property ROI Comparison: Saudi Arabia vs UAE

Jun 30, 2026 13 min read

Property ROI Comparison: Saudi Arabia vs UAE — Where Should Your Family Invest?

When a family decides to invest in property, the first question is not which apartment. It is which city. For Gulf families with ties to both Saudi Arabia and the UAE — and there are many — comparing investment returns across the two countries is not academic. It is a real financial decision with long consequences.

Here is the short answer: Saudi Arabia offers higher rental yields in its major cities (6–8% in Riyadh versus 5–8% in Dubai), lower purchase costs, and a booming market driven by Vision 2030. The UAE offers greater liquidity, a more mature secondary market, and stronger capital appreciation in premium districts. Which one suits your family depends on your budget, residency, and timeline.

This guide compares every dimension that matters to a family investor: rental income, capital growth, purchase costs, mortgage options, legal frameworks, and the neighbourhoods that offer the best returns for family living.

Rental Yields: Saudi Arabia vs UAE — The Numbers

Rental yield is the annual rent you collect divided by the purchase price. It is the closest thing real estate has to a dividend, and for family investors it is often the most important number. In Saudi Arabia's four largest cities — Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam, and Makkah — gross rental yields on apartments range from 6% to 9%. Villas yield slightly less, around 5% to 7%. Data from the Saudi Ministry of Justice's Ejar platform shows that average apartment rents in Riyadh rose roughly 12% year-on-year through early 2026, driven by population inflow and limited new supply in central districts.

In the UAE, Dubai's gross rental yields on apartments average between 5% and 8%, with the highest yields in affordable areas like International City (8%+) and the lowest in prime locations like Palm Jumeirah (3–4%). Abu Dhabi yields are lower: 4–6% for apartments and 5–7% for villas. Sharjah and Ajman offer higher yields (7–9%) but slower capital appreciation. The practical takeaway: a family buying a 1-million-SAR apartment in Riyadh can expect around 65,000–80,000 SAR in annual rental income. The same apartment in Dubai's mid-tier areas (costing roughly the same in AED terms) delivers comparable rental income but with higher transaction taxes and service charges.

Capital Appreciation: Where Is the Land Rising Fastest?

Rental income pays the bills. Capital appreciation builds the wealth. Over the past three years, Saudi real estate prices have shown strong upward momentum. According to the General Authority for Statistics (GASTAT), real estate prices in Saudi Arabia increased by about 4.2% in 2024 and an estimated 5–6% in 2025. Riyadh's residential prices have risen faster than the national average, with some districts seeing 8–12% annual appreciation since 2023.

Dubai's market is more cyclical. After a post-pandemic boom that saw residential prices rise over 20% in 2022–2023, growth slowed to around 6–8% in 2024 and is projected at 3–5% for 2026. Abu Dhabi has been steadier, with 3–4% annual appreciation over the same period. For a family, the difference matters: Saudi real estate is in the middle of a structural shift — massive urban expansion, new economic cities, and growing population. UAE markets are more mature and more dependent on global investor sentiment.

Purchase Costs: What You Actually Pay to Buy

The sticker price of a property is only the beginning. In Saudi Arabia, a buyer pays a 5% value-added tax on the purchase price plus a 2.5% real estate transaction fee through the Ejar and Wafi systems. Total upfront costs: roughly 7.5–8% above the purchase price. For a 1.5-million-SAR villa, that adds around 115,000 SAR in fees.

In Dubai, the buyer pays a 4% Dubai Land Department transfer fee, a 2% agency commission (negotiable), and a registration fee of 1,000–5,000 AED. Total upfront: roughly 6–6.5% above the purchase price. Additionally, Dubai properties carry annual service charges of 10–30 AED per square foot, which the owner pays regardless of occupancy. Saudi Arabia has no regular service charge regime for apartments — that cost is embedded in the rent. For villas in compounds, a small annual maintenance fee applies but is far lower than Dubai's standard charges.

The bottom line: upfront costs are lower in Saudi Arabia, and ongoing ownership costs (service charges, municipality fees) are significantly lower. The UAE compensates with a more efficient resale process and a deeper pool of buyers when you want to exit.

Mortgage Options: Islamic Finance in Both Markets

Both Saudi Arabia and the UAE offer Shariah-compliant mortgage products through their banking systems. In Saudi Arabia, SAMA regulates real estate financing with clear caps: first-time buyers can finance up to 90% of the property value (for homes up to 1.5 million SAR), and up to 85% for higher values. The maximum repayment period is 30 years, and total debt burden cannot exceed 45% of monthly income.

The Sakani program provides additional subsidies: reduced profit margins on the first 500,000 SAR of financing and monthly instalment support during the first five years. This is a genuine advantage for Saudi families that is not available in the UAE. In the UAE, Islamic mortgages (Murabaha, Ijara) are widely available. Expat buyers can typically finance 75–80% of the property value, with a 20–25% down payment. Mortgage tenures go up to 25 years, and monthly payments cannot exceed 50% of the borrower's income.

Best Neighbourhoods for Family Investments

Saudi Arabia

  • Riyadh — Al Malqa, Al Narjis, Al Yasmin: High family demand, good schools, yields 6–8%. 3-bed apartments: 700K–1.2M SAR.
  • Jeddah — Al Shati, Al Mohammadiyah: Coastal premium, yields 5–7%, stable demand. Villas: 1.5–3M SAR.
  • Dammam — Al Faisaliyah: Affordable, yields 7–9%. Strong Aramco employee demand.
  • Makkah — Al Aziziyah: Religious tourism drives 5–7% yields, seasonal fluctuation.

UAE

  • Dubai — JVC, Dubai Hills, Arabian Ranches: Family communities, yields 5–7%.
  • Dubai — Marina, Downtown: Higher appreciation but lower yields (4–5%).
  • Abu Dhabi — Al Reem, Saadiyat, Yas: 4–6% yields, strong expat demand.
  • Sharjah — Al Majaz: Lower entry (500K–1M AED for 2-bed), yields 7–9%.

Expat Ownership: A Crucial Difference

Expatriates can buy property in Saudi Arabia in designated investment areas, which now include most major cities under Vision 2030 reforms. However, expat ownership is restricted to specific districts and requires approval from the Ministry of Municipal and Rural Affairs. GCC nationals face fewer restrictions and can buy in most areas.

In the UAE, expats can buy freehold property in designated areas in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, and Sharjah. Freehold ownership gives full title in Dubai, while Abu Dhabi offers 99-year leasehold in some areas. The process is straightforward and transparent — one reason expat investors prefer the UAE. For a family deciding between the two countries, residency status is the deciding factor. If you are a Saudi or GCC national, Saudi Arabia offers better returns and lower costs. If you are a long-term expat, the UAE provides clearer ownership rights.

Tax Implications: The Quiet Cost Difference

Saudi Arabia has no property tax and no capital gains tax on real estate. The only recurring government cost is the annual municipality fee of about 1,000–2,000 SAR. The UAE also has no property tax or capital gains tax, but Dubai charges an annual housing fee of 5% of the property's annual rental value (calculated as 5% of the DLD valuation). For a 1.5M AED apartment rented at 90,000 AED annually, that is 4,500 AED per year.

The Verdict: Where Should Your Family Invest?

Saudi nationals and GCC families should lean toward Saudi Arabia. The rental yields are higher, purchase costs are lower, and the market is in a structural growth phase. Riyadh and Jeddah offer the best combination of yield and appreciation. Expat families with long-term Gulf plans should consider the UAE for the clarity of ownership and liquidity of the resale market. Dubai's freehold system is proven, and the quality of family communities is world-class. For families with maximum flexibility, diversify: a buy-to-let property in Riyadh for ongoing income, and a smaller apartment in Dubai for appreciation.

FAQ

Which city gives better rental yield for family apartments?

Riyadh and Dubai are comparable, with Riyadh slightly ahead (6–8% vs 5–8%). Riyadh's lower purchase costs and service charges give it a higher net yield.

Can GCC nationals buy property in Dubai?

Yes, GCC nationals can buy freehold property anywhere in Dubai with the same ownership rights as UAE nationals.

Is Saudi real estate cheaper than UAE?

On a per-square-metre basis, Saudi real estate is roughly 30–50% cheaper. A 3-bed apartment in Riyadh costs 700K–1M SAR, while a similar unit in Dubai costs 1.2M–2M SAR.

What are hidden costs of buying in Dubai?

4% DLD transfer fee, 2% agency commission, annual service charges (10–30 AED/sqft), and 5% annual housing fee. Combined: 7–10% over five years.

Is off-plan investment safe for families?

Off-plan carries construction delay and developer risk. For a first property, ready-to-move-in is safer. Off-plan works for additional investment with established developers and escrow protection.
