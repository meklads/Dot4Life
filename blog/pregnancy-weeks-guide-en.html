Pregnancy Weeks Guide

2026-06-088 min read

Introduction

According to WHO, Pregnancy is a remarkable 40-week journey divided into three trimesters, each with distinct developmental milestones, physical changes, and emotional experiences. Whether you are a first-time mother in Riyadh or expecting your third child in Dubai, understanding what happens each week helps you prepare for what lies ahead, and know when something needs medical attention.

This guide covers fetal development week by week, key maternal symptoms, and practical advice tailored for Gulf families, including prenatal care schedules followed by the Saudi Ministry of Health and UAE health authorities.

Key Numbers

40 weeks = full-term pregnancy · 3 trimesters · 8-12 prenatal visits recommended · Due date = LMP + 280 days

First Trimester (Weeks 1-12)

The first trimester is the most critical period for fetal development. All major organs begin forming, and the baby grows from a single fertilized cell to a fully-formed fetus measuring about 5-6 cm by week 12. This is also when most miscarriages occur, approximately 10-15% of confirmed pregnancies.

Weeks 1-4: Conception & Implantation

Pregnancy is counted from the first day of your last period. Around week 2-3, ovulation occurs and fertilization happens. The fertilized egg travels to the uterus and implants into the uterine lining. Many women don't know they are pregnant yet. Early symptoms may include light spotting, fatigue, and breast tenderness.

Weeks 5-8: Heartbeat & Organ Formation

The baby's heart starts beating around week 6, often visible on ultrasound. The brain, spinal cord, and neural tube form. Arms and leg buds appear. Morning sickness typically begins around week 6 and peaks at week 9. In GCC clinics, the first prenatal visit usually happens around week 6-8 for a viability scan.

Weeks 9-12: From Embryo to Fetus

The baby's organs continue maturing. Fingers and toes form. The face takes shape with eyes, nose, and mouth. By week 12, the risk of miscarriage drops significantly. The first trimester screening (including nuchal translucency ultrasound) is performed between week 11-13 in Saudi and UAE hospitals.

Second Trimester (Weeks 13-27)

Often called the "golden period" of pregnancy. Morning sickness usually fades, energy returns, and the baby bump becomes visible. The mother can feel fetal movements (quickening) starting around week 18-22. This trimester includes the crucial anomaly scan at week 18-22.

Weeks 13-16: Rapid Growth

The baby grows rapidly, doubling in size. The placenta takes over hormone production. Hair begins to grow on the head. The mother's uterus expands and the pregnancy becomes visible. Many GCC women report feeling best during this period.

Weeks 17-22: Movement & Anatomy Scan

Most mothers feel the first kicks between week 18-22. The mid-pregnancy ultrasound (anomaly scan) at week 18-22 checks all major organs, measures growth, and can reveal the baby's sex. In Saudi private hospitals, this is the most anticipated scan for families.

Weeks 23-27: Viability & Glucose Test

Babies born after week 24 have a chance of survival with intensive care. The glucose tolerance test (GTT) for gestational diabetes is performed between week 24-28. Gestational diabetes affects 15-20% of pregnancies in the GCC, one of the highest rates globally, making this test essential.

Third Trimester (Weeks 28-40)

The final stretch, the baby gains weight rapidly, organs mature, and the body prepares for birth. Discomfort increases as the baby grows. Visits to the doctor become more frequent, every 2 weeks from week 28, then weekly from week 36.

Weeks 28-32: Rapid Weight Gain

The baby gains significant weight and fat stores. Brain development accelerates. The mother may experience shortness of breath, back pain, and difficulty sleeping. In Gulf summers, staying hydrated becomes especially challenging, aim for 2.5-3 litres of water daily.

Weeks 33-36: Final Preparations

The baby settles into a head-down position (engagement). Braxton Hicks contractions increase. The mother should prepare the hospital bag and finalize the birth plan. The GBS (Group B Streptococcus) test is done around week 36. In Saudi hospitals, birth registration paperwork through the "Absher" system should be arranged.

Weeks 37-40: Full Term & Birth

Babies born at 37 weeks are considered early-term but generally healthy. Full term is 39-40 weeks. Signs of labor include regular contractions, water breaking (amniotic fluid rupture), and bloody show. In the GCC, planned C-sections are commonly scheduled around week 38-39. Only about 5% of babies arrive exactly on their due date, most come within two weeks before or after.

When to Call Your Doctor

Severe abdominal pain · Heavy bleeding · Severe headache with vision changes · Fever over 38°C · No fetal movement · Water breaking before 37 weeks · Persistent vomiting

Frequently Asked Questions

How is pregnancy week calculated?

Pregnancy is calculated from the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP), not conception. A full-term pregnancy is 40 weeks (280 days). Due date = LMP + 280 days.

What are the three trimesters?

First trimester: week 1-12 (organ formation). Second trimester: week 13-27 (growth, movement). Third trimester: week 28-40 (rapid growth, preparation for birth).

When does fetal movement start?

Most mothers feel movement between week 18-25. First-time mothers may not feel it until week 25; second-time mothers often feel it by week 16.

How many prenatal visits are needed?

The Saudi MOH recommends 8-12 visits: monthly until week 28, every 2 weeks from week 28-36, and weekly from week 36 to birth.

Conclusion

Every pregnancy is unique, your experience may differ from the descriptions above. What matters most is regular prenatal care, listening to your body, and building a support system of family and healthcare professionals. The GCC has excellent maternity healthcare, whether public (MOH, medical cities) or private (Al-Habib, Saudi German, Mediclinic). Practical Tips for Pregnancy Weeks Guide

When it comes to Pregnancy Weeks Guide, Gulf families can benefit greatly from following a structured approach that takes into account their unique needs, cultural values, and lifestyle preferences. Start by identifying your family's specific requirements and priorities, then research available options thoroughly before making any decisions or commitments that could affect your family's budget, time, and overall well-being.

Planning ahead is essential for success. Create a clear roadmap that outlines your goals, timeline, and resources needed to achieve the best possible outcomes for your family. Consider factors such as seasonal changes, school schedules, work commitments, and religious observances that may impact your plans and choices throughout the year.

Involving all family members in the decision-making process from an early stage helps ensure everyone feels valued and invested in the outcome. Hold regular family discussions to share ideas, address concerns, and reach consensus on important matters. This collaborative approach not only leads to better decisions but also strengthens family bonds and teaches children valuable skills in communication, negotiation, and compromise that will benefit them throughout their lives.

Conclusion

Use a week-by-week tracker, write questions before each prenatal visit, and share updates with your partner early so decisions are made calmly, not under delivery-room pressure.

To make the most of this guide on Pregnancy Weeks Guide, Gulf families should consider creating a personalized action plan that addresses their specific circumstances, goals, and priorities. Every family has unique needs based on their size, income level, location, lifestyle, and cultural values. By tailoring the general advice in this article to your particular situation, you can achieve better outcomes and create a more meaningful and rewarding experience for every member of your family from the youngest to the oldest.

One effective approach is to start with small, manageable steps rather than trying to implement everything at once. Focus on the most important changes that will have the greatest positive impact on your family's well-being and quality of life. As you build momentum and confidence, gradually introduce additional improvements and refinements to your family routines, systems, and habits. Remember that consistency and patience are key to achieving lasting positive change that benefits everyone in the family unit and creates a stronger, more resilient family bond that can weather any challenge or difficulty that life may bring your way in the future.

Another important consideration is seeking support and guidance from qualified professionals when needed. Whether it is a financial advisor, healthcare provider, educational consultant, religious scholar, or family counselor, having access to expert advice can make a significant difference in your family's success and satisfaction with the decisions you make together. Do not hesitate to ask questions, seek clarification, and request additional resources when you need them to make informed choices.
