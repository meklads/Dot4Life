How to Calculate Zakat on Money and Gold 2025, Complete Guide

8 min read

Introduction

According to SAMA, Every Ramadan, millions of Muslims across the Gulf and around the world ask the same question: How do I calculate my Zakat accurately? With multiple asset types in the modern era, bank savings and gold to stocks, investment funds, and even digital assets, calculating Zakat has become far more complex than it once was.

Zakat is the Third Pillar of Islam, a mandatory financial act of worship designed to purify wealth and ensure social solidarity. Allah says in the Quran: "Take from their wealth a charity to purify and sanctify them" (At-Tawbah 9:103). This comprehensive guide walks you through how to calculate Zakat on your wealth in 2025 with practical examples and current Nisab values in SAR, AED, and KWD.

Quick Summary

Zakat = 2.5% of net wealth that has reached the Nisab threshold and been held for one lunar year (Hawl). The 2025 Nisab is approximately SAR 21,250 or AED 20,800 or KWD 1,700 (depending on gold price).

Understanding Nisab, The Threshold

Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth that makes Zakat obligatory. It was set by Shariah as the equivalent of 85 grams of gold or 595 grams of silver. Most contemporary scholars recommend using the gold standard because it is more stable and aligned with modern economic realities.

Since gold prices fluctuate daily, the Nisab changes accordingly. Here are approximate Nisab values for 2025:

Saudi Riyal (SAR) SAR 21,250 SAR 250
UAE Dirham (AED) AED 20,800 AED 245
Kuwaiti Dinar (KWD) KWD 1,700 KWD 20

What Assets Are Zakatable?

Zakat is due on six main categories of wealth, with modern adaptations:

1. Cash Savings: All money you hold in bank accounts or at home, checking accounts, savings accounts, fixed deposits. Zakat is due on the full amount if it reaches the Nisab and one full lunar year has passed, at 2.5%.

2. Gold and Silver: If you own 85 grams or more of gold (or 595g of silver) and one year has passed, Zakat is due at 2.5% of current market value. For gold jewelry worn regularly, scholars differ. The majority view is that Zakat is still due if it reaches the Nisab and a full year passes.

3. Trade Goods (Business Inventory): All items held for sale, retail inventory, raw materials, finished products. Valued at selling price at year-end, Zakat at 2.5%.

4. Stocks and Investment Funds: For listed stocks, Zakat is calculated on your share of the company's zakatable assets (cash, receivables, inventory), not the market price. GCC-listed companies typically disclose Zakat-per-share values. For ETFs and money market funds, 2.5% on total value.

5. Investment Real Estate: Properties held for rental income, no Zakat on the property value itself. Zakat is due on net rental income if it reaches Nisab and the hawl is complete. Properties held for resale (real estate trading) are treated as trade goods.

6. Receivable Debts: Money you have lent to others that is expected to be repaid. If the borrower is solvent, include it in your Zakat base. If the borrower is insolvent, Zakat is due only upon actual receipt.

Step-by-Step Calculation Example

Meet Ahmed. He has the following assets and a full lunar year has passed:

Asset Value Zakatable Amount Zakat Due
Bank savings SAR 50,000 SAR 50,000 SAR 1,250
Gold (100g × SAR 250) SAR 25,000 SAR 25,000 SAR 625
Stocks (zakatable assets = 40%) SAR 30,000 SAR 12,000 SAR 300
Total SAR 105,000 SAR 87,000 SAR 2,175

Steps:

1. Gather all your zakatable assets (cash, gold, investments).
2. Check if the total exceeds Nisab (below ~SAR 21,250 = no Zakat due).
3. Confirm the assets have been held for one full lunar year (354 days).
4. Exclude personal-use assets (home, car, furniture).
5. Calculate 2.5% of the zakatable amount: multiply by 0.025.

Zakat Formula
Zakat = (Total zakatable assets) × 0.025

Example: 87,000 × 0.025 = SAR 2,175

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Nisab threshold for Zakat in 2025?
The Nisab is equivalent to 85 grams of gold. At approximately $60 per gram, the Nisab is around $5,100 USD or 19,000 SAR. This changes with gold prices.

Is Zakat due on monthly salary?
Zakat is not due on salary at the time of receipt. It is due on savings that have reached the Nisab and been held for a full lunar year (Hawl). If you save from your salary and the savings reach Nisab and remain for a year, Zakat is due.

How do I calculate Zakat on gold?
If you own 85 grams or more of gold for a full lunar year, you pay 2.5% of its current market value as Zakat. Example: 100g × $60 = $6,000. Zakat = $6,000 × 0.025 = $150.

Is Zakat due on gold jewelry worn regularly?
Scholars differ on this. The majority opinion holds that Zakat is due on gold jewelry if it reaches the Nisab and a full year passes, regardless of whether it is worn. Some scholars exempt jewelry that is worn. The cautious position is to pay Zakat.

What is the difference between Zakat al-Mal and Zakat al-Fitr?
Zakat al-Mal is an annual 2.5% obligation on wealth above the Nisab held for one lunar year. Zakat al-Fitr is a fixed amount per person paid at the end of Ramadan, approximately 2.5 kg of staple food per person, due before Eid prayer.

Practical Decision Framework

When planning your family's Islamic practice:
1. Calculate Zakat annually on the same date - use the Hijri calendar to maintain consistency. Track all asset types: cash, gold, investments, and business inventory.
2. Plan Umrah during off-peak months - Dhul Qadah, Muharram, and Safar offer lower costs, shorter queues, and milder weather while maintaining spiritual merit.
3. Structure family prayers as group activity - even 10 minutes of family Quran reading between Maghrib and Isha builds consistent habits in children.
4. For inheritance planning, document intentions early - Islamic inheritance (ilm al-faraid) has specific share distributions that are easier to implement when planned in advance.

Common mistake: Delaying Umrah or Hajj planning until the last minute. Booking 6-8 months ahead for Umrah during Ramadan can save 40-60% on packages, with better hotel proximity to Al-Haram.

Zakat is more than a religious obligation, it is an economic system that purifies wealth, encourages growth, and ensures society's most vulnerable are supported.
