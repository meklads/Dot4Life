Islamic Inheritance Basics

2026-06-088 min read

A Duty Most Families Postpone

Islamic inheritance (Mawarith) is among the most precisely detailed areas of Sharia, and among the most postponed in practice. Distributing an estate by guesswork or by "what feels fair" can wrong heirs and create lasting family disputes. Understanding the basics protects both your faith and your family.

In the Quran, Allah dedicates detailed verses to inheritance in Surah An-Nisa (4:11-12, 4:176), emphasizing its importance. The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) said, "Learn the laws of inheritance and teach them, for they are half of knowledge" (Ibn Majah). Despite this strong emphasis, many Muslim families delay estate planning until a crisis occurs. By then, emotions run high, memories of exact asset details fade, and disagreements become harder to resolve.

Fixed Shares Come First

Before anything is divided, the estate settles debts and any valid bequest (up to one-third for non-heirs). Then come the fixed shares (furud) set in the Quran, for example a husband, wife, daughters, and parents each have defined portions. What remains passes to the residuary heirs (asaba). The Fixed Share Holders and Their Portions

The Quran specifies eight categories of fixed-share heirs. The husband receives 1/4 of his wife's estate if they have children, or 1/2 if they do not. The wife receives 1/8 of her husband's estate if they have children, or 1/4 if they do not. Multiple wives share the wife's portion equally. Daughters receive 1/2 each if there is only one daughter, or 2/3 collectively if there are two or more - unless there is a son, in which case the son's share is twice the daughter's (the well-known "2:1" ratio). The father receives 1/6 if there is a son, or may inherit more if there is no son. The mother receives 1/6 if there are children or multiple siblings, or 1/3 otherwise. Full siblings, half-siblings from the father's side, and grandparents also have defined shares in specific scenarios.

Common Inheritance Scenarios

Scenario 1: Husband Dies Leaving a Wife and Children

In this common situation, the wife receives 1/8 of the estate. The children inherit the remainder, with sons receiving twice the share of daughters. The parents of the deceased may also inherit 1/6 each if alive. For example, on an estate of SAR 800,000: wife receives SAR 100,000 (1/8), father receives SAR 133,333 (1/6), mother receives SAR 133,333 (1/6), and the remaining SAR 433,334 is divided among the children at a 2:1 ratio for sons versus daughters. If there are two sons and one daughter, each son receives approximately SAR 173,334 and the daughter receives SAR 86,666.

Scenario 2: Parents Die Without Children

When someone dies without children, their spouse and siblings become key heirs. The husband or wife receives their fixed share, and siblings inherit the remainder. Full siblings receive priority over half-siblings. If the deceased is survived only by a spouse and full siblings, the spouse receives their share and the siblings divide the rest. This scenario highlights why inheritance planning is especially important for couples without children - without clear documentation, disputes among siblings are more likely.

When Shares Shift: Complex Situations

Islamic inheritance becomes more complex when specific combinations of heirs are present. The presence of a grandfather alongside brothers can fundamentally change the distribution. Multiple wives, stepchildren, or heirs from different marriages add further complexity. The doctrine of Al-Awl (proportional reduction) applies when the sum of fixed shares exceeds the estate - all shares are proportionally reduced. Conversely, Ar-Radd (return) applies when there is a surplus after fixed shares are distributed, returning the excess to eligible heirs. These scenarios demonstrate why manual calculations are prone to error and why our inheritance calculator provides a reliable starting point.

📝 Important Note on Wills

In Islamic law, a valid will (wasiyyah) cannot exceed one-third of the estate, and cannot be made in favor of an heir who already inherits under Sharia. However, in many Gulf countries, expatriates may also be subject to their home country's inheritance laws for assets located abroad. It is essential to consult both Sharia principles and local legal requirements when drafting an Islamic will. Many Gulf law firms now offer Islamic will-writing services that comply with both Sharia and civil law.

Why a Calculator Helps, But a Scholar Decides

Combinations of heirs change the shares in ways that are easy to get wrong by hand, the presence of a son, a grandfather, or multiple wives shifts everything. A calculator gives you a fast, accurate starting estimate. For the final, binding distribution, especially with complex families, estates, or businesses, refer to a qualified scholar or Sharia court.

Even a seemingly simple estate can have hidden complexities. Business ownership shares, outstanding loans, jointly owned property, and assets in multiple countries all affect the distributable estate. Debts must be settled first, including funeral expenses, outstanding loans, and unpaid zakat calculator. Only then is the remaining estate divided among heirs. Our inheritance calculator helps you understand the structure and estimate shares, but it does not replace formal legal and religious consultation. For complex estates - especially those involving ongoing businesses or international assets - appointing a qualified executor and documenting a clear Islamic will is essential.

Conclusion: Act Now, Before It's Needed

Islamic inheritance is not just a legal process - it is an act of worship and a final responsibility toward your family. The Quran's detailed guidance on this matter reflects its importance in preserving family harmony and fulfilling the rights of every heir. By understanding the basics, using available tools like our inheritance calculator, and consulting qualified scholars for your specific situation, you can fulfill this duty properly. Do not postpone this essential obligation. Document your assets, discuss your wishes with your family, and create a Sharia-compliant will. Your family will thank you for the clarity and peace of mind your planning provides.

Disclaimer: This article is general information, not a religious ruling (fatwa). Scholars may differ on specifics; consult qualified scholars and official sources for your own situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the basic share of a wife in Islamic inheritance?

A wife receives 1/8 of her deceased husband's estate if they have children, and 1/4 if they have no children. This share is prescribed in the Quran (Surah An-Nisa, 4:12). If there are multiple wives, they share equally in this portion.

How is inheritance distributed among children in Islam?

The general principle is that a son receives twice the share of a daughter. This is based on the Quranic ruling in Surah An-Nisa (4:11). The son's larger share reflects his financial responsibility in Islamic law to support his family and, if needed, his sisters.

Can I write a will to distribute my wealth differently from Sharia?

Under Sharia, you can distribute up to 1/3 of your estate through a will (wasiyyah) to non-Quranic heirs or charitable causes. The remaining 2/3 must be distributed according to fixed shares. This 1/3 limit ensures Quranic heirs receive their prescribed portions.

What happens if someone dies without a will in Islam?

Without a will, the estate is distributed according to Sharia inheritance rules under Islamic law. For Muslim families in Gulf countries, Sharia courts typically oversee this distribution. The shares are fixed: spouse, children, parents, and other eligible relatives receive their prescribed portions.

Do debts and funeral expenses come before inheritance?

Yes, in Islamic law, the following take priority: funeral expenses, then debts (including unpaid zakat), then the will (up to 1/3), and finally the remaining estate is distributed to heirs according to Sharia shares. This order is based on Quranic instruction and scholarly consensus.
