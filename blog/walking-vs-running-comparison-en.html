Walking vs Running: Which Burns More Calories?

2026-06-08 8 min read

Calories Burned Per Minute

Running burns approximately 10–14 calories per minute for a 70 kg person at a moderate pace (8 km/h). Walking at a brisk pace (5 km/h) burns approximately 4–6 calories per minute. So running burns about 2.5 times more calories per minute than walking.

The actual number depends on several factors beyond weight and pace. Your age, body composition, running economy, and even terrain affect calorie burn. Running on a 5% incline can increase calorie burn by 50% compared to flat ground. Walking uphill has a similar proportional effect. The key insight: if you are short on time, running delivers more calorie burn per minute. But if you have time to spare, walking can achieve comparable total calorie burn over a longer duration - with significantly less stress on your body.

Total Calories: The Distance Factor

If you cover the same distance, the calorie difference narrows significantly. Walking 5 km burns approximately 250–300 calories. Running 5 km burns approximately 350–400 calories. The difference is about 30%, not 2.5× - because walking simply takes longer.

This is a crucial insight for anyone planning an exercise routine. If your primary goal is calorie burn and you have 45 minutes, running will burn more. But if you can walk for 90 minutes, you might burn the same or more calories as a 45-minute run. Over a week, the total volume matters more than the intensity of any single session. Many people find they can walk daily with ease, while running requires rest days - making weekly walking totals potentially higher than weekly running totals for beginners.

💡 The Sweet Spot

For most adults, a combination works best: walk daily for baseline activity and metabolic health, and add 2–3 running sessions per week for cardiovascular fitness and calorie burning.

Cardiovascular Benefits Compared

Both walking and running improve cardiovascular health, but they do so through different mechanisms. Running strengthens the heart muscle more aggressively, increasing stroke volume and lowering resting heart rate more rapidly. Studies show that runners have a 30–45% lower risk of cardiovascular disease compared to non-runners. However, walking at a brisk pace - achieving 60–70% of maximum heart rate - provides similar long-term cardiovascular benefits. A landmark study published in the American Heart Association journal found that walking at a brisk pace for 30 minutes daily reduced cardiovascular risk by 31%, only slightly less than running's 38% risk reduction. The key variable is total energy expenditure, not exercise intensity. For those who cannot run due to age, joint issues, or medical conditions, brisk walking offers excellent cardiovascular protection with minimal risk.

Sustainability & Joint Impact

Running generates 3–4 times your body weight in impact force with each stride. For overweight individuals or those with joint issues, this can lead to injury. Walking generates only 1.2–1.5 times body weight and is far more sustainable for daily activity.

Injury rates tell a clear story. Research shows that 50–80% of runners experience at least one injury per year, most commonly affecting the knees, shins, and feet. Runner's knee, shin splints, plantar fasciitis, and Achilles tendonitis are the most frequent complaints. Walkers have injury rates of just 1–5% annually, making walking one of the safest physical activities. For Gulf families where sedentary lifestyles are common, starting with walking is the safest path to building an exercise habit. After 2–3 months of regular walking, transitioning to a run-walk program can safely prepare the body for running. Proper footwear, gradual progression, and listening to your body are essential regardless of which activity you choose.

Mental Health and Mood Benefits

Both activities significantly improve mental health, but through different pathways. Running triggers a well-documented "runner's high" - the release of endorphins and endocannabinoids that create feelings of euphoria and reduced anxiety. This effect is particularly strong at moderate to high intensities lasting 20+ minutes. Walking, on the other hand, promotes mindfulness, stress reduction, and creative thinking. Many writers and thinkers throughout history - from Aristotle to Nietzsche - relied on walking for mental clarity. Recent research confirms that a 30-minute walk in nature reduces cortisol levels by 18% and improves mood in 82% of participants. For Gulf residents with access to walkable neighborhoods or parks, incorporating a daily 20–30 minute walk can significantly improve mental wellbeing without the physical demands of running.

Time Efficiency: The Practical Factor

For busy Gulf professionals and parents, time is often the limiting factor. A 5 km run takes about 30–35 minutes at a moderate pace. The same distance walked takes 50–60 minutes. Over a week, running four times saves 1–2 hours compared to walking the same distance. However, walking integrates more easily into daily routine - walking to the mosque, parking further from the office entrance, taking a 15-minute post-lunch walk, or using stairs instead of the elevator. These "incidental" walking minutes add up without requiring dedicated exercise time. Smart watches and fitness trackers can help track both purposeful and incidental activity, making it easier to hit daily movement goals regardless of whether you run or walk.

Which Should You Choose?

Walk if you're new to exercise, have joint concerns, or want daily movement you can sustain. Run if you're time-pressed, already fit, or want maximum cardiovascular benefits. Better yet - do both on different days. A typical weekly split: 4–5 days of walking (with one longer weekend walk), and 2–3 days of running. This balanced approach maximizes the benefits of both while minimizing injury risk. Use our tools and trackers to monitor your progress and stay motivated.

Which Fits Your Week

The World Health Organization recommends adults get at least 150 minutes of moderate activity, such as brisk walking, or 75 minutes of vigorous activity like running, each week. That single benchmark reframes the debate: running reaches the target in less time, while walking is gentler on the knees and far easier to sustain for years. Neither is wrong; they simply ask different things of your schedule and your joints.

For most Gulf families, the climate decides as much as the calorie count. Early mornings and indoor malls make brisk walking realistic almost year-round, while running may suit cooler months or air-conditioned tracks. The most effective plan is the one you actually repeat, so many people blend the two: walk on busy days, run when energy and time allow.

Disclaimer: This article is general information only, not medical advice. Every body is different; consult your doctor or a qualified professional before changing your diet or exercise routine.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which burns more calories: walking or running?

Running burns approximately 2.5x more calories per minute than walking. For a 70kg person, running at 8 km/h burns about 8 calories/minute, while walking at 5 km/h burns about 3.5 calories/minute. However, over the same distance, the calorie difference is smaller - running burns only about 30% more calories per kilometer than walking.

Is walking enough for cardiovascular fitness?

Yes, brisk walking (5-6 km/h) provides excellent cardiovascular benefits for most people. The American Heart Association recommends 150 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise (brisk walking) or 75 minutes of vigorous exercise (running) weekly. For weight loss, walking is effective when combined with dietary changes, though running achieves results faster.

Which is better for joint health?

Walking is significantly lower impact and better for joint health. Running generates 3-4x body weight in impact force on each step, while walking generates only 1-1.5x body weight. For people with arthritis, knee pain, or recovering from injury, walking is the safer choice. Running on soft surfaces (grass, trails) reduces impact.

Can walking help with weight loss as much as running?

Running produces faster weight loss results due to higher calorie burn and greater afterburn effect (EPOC). However, walking is more sustainable for most people and can produce significant weight loss over time. A 80kg person walking 30 minutes daily at brisk pace burns about 1,800 extra calories monthly - enough to lose about 0.5kg per month without dietary changes.

What is the optimal walking speed for health benefits?

Brisk walking at 5-6.5 km/h (100-120 steps per minute for adults) provides optimal cardiovascular benefits. At this pace, you should be breathing harder but still able to hold a conversation. For weight loss, intervals of 3 minutes fast walking + 2 minutes moderate walking are more effective than a steady pace.
