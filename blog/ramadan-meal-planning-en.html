Ramadan Meal Planning

2026-06-088 min read

The Paradox: A Month of Fasting Where Many Gain Weight

Research published in the Journal of Nutrition in 2024 analyzed 137 studies on Ramadan fasting across 22 countries and found a striking result: about 40% of fasting adults gain weight during Ramadan, while 35% lose weight, and 25% remain stable. The weight-gain group shared a common pattern, calorie intake at Iftar and the late-night Suhoor meal exceeded their total daily expenditure because of calorie-dense, high-sugar foods consumed rapidly after a long fast.

Key Insights — Citation-Ready

Islamic financial obligations are often misunderstood and under-calculated by Gulf families. Zakat is not merely a charitable donation — it is a compulsory act of worship with precise calculation rules that vary by asset type, holding period, and applicable nisab threshold. For 2025, the nisab threshold based on silver is approximately 2,975 SAR or 2,910 AED. Many families overpay or underpay simply because they do not track their zakat-eligible assets systematically throughout the year.

Suhoor: The Meal That Determines Your Entire Day

Suhoor is the most metabolically important meal of the fasting day. Its composition determines your energy level, mental clarity, and hunger intensity for the following 14–18 hours. The research is clear on three principles:

1. Slow-release carbohydrates, not rice or bread
White rice, white bread, and sweetened cereals cause a blood sugar spike followed by a crash within 2–3 hours. Oats, lentils, whole grain bread, sweet potato, and legumes release energy slowly over 4–6 hours. A suhoor with oats, eggs, and dates keeps you satiated up to 4x longer than a white rice meal of the same calories.
2. Protein is your hunger suppressant
Protein activates satiety hormones (GLP-1, PYY) that signal fullness to the brain for hours. Aim for 25–35g of protein at Suhoor: 2–3 eggs, 200g Greek yogurt, a handful of nuts and cheese, or a protein-rich lentil soup. Families in the MENA region who added protein to Suhoor reported significantly lower fatigue and irritability during fasting hours in a 2023 Saudi study.
3. Hydration before sunrise, not just water
Dehydration during fasting in Gulf heat (where temperatures reach 42–48°C in summer Ramadans) is a major driver of headaches and mental fog. Drink 2–3 glasses of water with electrolytes at Suhoor, not just plain water. Adding a pinch of salt, a squeeze of lemon, and 1 teaspoon of honey to a glass of water mimics an oral rehydration solution and helps retain fluids 3x longer than water alone.

Iftar: Break the Fast, Don't Break Your Metabolism

The Prophet's Sunnah of breaking fast with dates and water is nutritionally perfect. Dates provide fast-release glucose that stabilizes blood sugar before a full meal. Water rehydrates. Then the stomach signals readiness for more. The 15–20 minute pause between dates and the main meal is one of the most evidence-supported eating practices in nutrition science, it prevents overeating by allowing satiety signals to reach the brain before you've already consumed 2,000 calories.

What to Minimize at Iftar
ما يجب تقليله عند الإفطار

Fried foods (samboosa, spring rolls, fried chicken) consumed on an empty stomach after 16 hours of fasting cause an extreme insulin spike and accelerated fat storage. They're not forbidden, but having them as a small portion after a protein-and-vegetable main course dramatically reduces their metabolic damage. Sweet drinks (Vimto, sweetened juices, qamarddin) add 200–400 empty calories before you've even started eating. Water, laban (buttermilk), or a small glass of fresh juice is superior.

The Ideal Ramadan Day: A Simple Template

  • Suhoor (30 min before Fajr): Oats with nuts + 2 eggs or Greek yogurt + 1–2 dates + 2 glasses water with electrolytes
  • Iftar (at Maghrib): 1–3 dates + water → pause 15 min → main meal: grilled protein + vegetables + moderate carbs
  • Late evening meal (optional): Light, fruit, nuts, yogurt. Avoid eating within 2 hours of sleep.
