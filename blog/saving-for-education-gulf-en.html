Saving for Education in the Gulf

2026-06-088 min read

The Number That Surprises Every Gulf Parent

Private school from KG through grade 12 in the Gulf can exceed 600,000 SAR per child (depending on the city and school), and a four-year university degree abroad , in the UK, US, Malaysia, or even the UAE , can match or exceed that figure again. A family with three children faces an education bill that could surpass 3.6 million SAR, before factoring in education inflation, which typically outpaces general inflation by about two to three percent annually.

The good news: parents who start early need to save a fraction of what late starters do. Time , not income , does most of the work through the power of compound returns. The earlier you start, the smaller the monthly amount needed.

The Power of Compound Interest

Scenario A , Starting from birth: Save 1,000 SAR per month from birth, at a modest five percent annual return (a balanced investment portfolio), and you reach approximately 290,000 SAR by age 18. Your total contributions: 216,000 SAR. The rest is compounded growth.

Scenario B , Starting at age 10: To reach the same 290,000 SAR goal in only 8 years, you need to save approximately 2,500-3,000 SAR per month , three times the monthly amount. That is the cost of missing 8 years of compound growth.

Scenario C , Starting at age 15: You would need over 5,000 SAR per month. At this point, saving becomes unrealistic for most families, forcing reliance on loans or limiting educational options.

The lesson is clear: every year of delay costs more than you think. Use our savings-goal calculator to test different scenarios based on your children's ages and your target.

Savings and Investment Options in the Gulf

In Saudi Arabia and the wider Gulf region, several tools are suitable for long-term education savings:

  • Low-cost ETFs and index funds: Available through licensed platforms (Derayah, Al Rajhi Capital, Sahm, etc.), offering diversification at low cost. A roughly three-fifths equities and two-fifths sukuk/bonds portfolio is appropriate for a 10-18 year horizon.
  • Islamic savings accounts: Gulf Islamic banks offer savings accounts with annual returns (currently around three to five percent). Suitable for short-term needs (under 5 years) but lose value to inflation over longer periods.
  • Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs): Income-producing and relatively low-volatility, suitable for diversifying an education savings portfolio.
  • School prepayment plans: Some private schools offer discounts of about ten to fifteen percent for annual or multi-year advance payments. Calculate the effective discount before committing.

Avoid holding education savings in a regular current account for many years; inflation erodes purchasing power. Education fee inflation in the Gulf often outpaces general inflation. Before investing, review licensed-product disclosures on the Capital Market Authority (CMA) site and consumer guidance from SAMA.

A Simple Three-Account System

Separate your children's education goal so daily life expenses don't consume it. The recommended system:

  • 1) Automatic monthly transfer: On salary day, before you see the money. Automation beats willpower every time. Pick a realistic amount and make it automatic.
  • 2) Dedicated education account: Do not mix with emergency funds or leisure accounts. Separating accounts prevents "borrowing" from your children's future.
  • 3) Diversified, low-cost investment: For long horizons (10+ years), cash alone loses to inflation. A diversified portfolio of equities, sukuk, and real estate can deliver positive real returns over 15-18 years.

Protect the Plan From Lifestyle Creep

Every salary raise is a fork in the road: upgrade the car or home, or upgrade your children's future. The practical rule: direct at least one-third of every raise straight to the education savings transfer before you adjust your spending to the new income. You will not miss money you never started spending. This single rule , if followed consistently , could fully fund one child's education without you ever feeling the pinch.

Education Inflation: Your Silent Enemy

When planning long-term education savings (15-18 years), you must account for education inflation. While general inflation in the Gulf runs at about two to four percent annually, education inflation is estimated at about five to eight percent per year. This means that a school fee of 50,000 SAR today could become 100,000-140,000 SAR in 12 years, and over 160,000 SAR in 18 years. For this reason, relying on a regular savings account yielding about three percent is insufficient , its real return after education inflation is negative. Investing in assets that grow at a rate exceeding education inflation (about seven percent or more annually) is the only way to preserve your education savings' purchasing power over the long term.

How to Build a Plan for Multiple Children

If you have more than one child, planning becomes more complex but not impossible. Here is a practical framework:

  • Prioritize by age: Older children (closer to university) need funding priority because their time horizon is shorter. Younger children can benefit from a longer savings runway.
  • Use one account with multiple portfolios: Instead of a separate account for each child, use one investment portfolio with a staggered withdrawal plan based on their university entry order.
  • Distribute monthly contributions by proximity: about two-fifths for the oldest, roughly one-third for the middle, and about one-quarter for the youngest (depending on age gaps). Rebalance over time.
  • Don't neglect other financial goals: Education savings are important, but not at the expense of retirement or emergency funds. Balance is key.

Cost Comparison: Public vs Private vs Overseas Education

Choosing your children's educational path significantly impacts your savings plan. Here is a rough cost comparison for the Gulf region:

  • Public education (free): Available to citizens in Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, and Kuwait. Quality varies but is generally good. Minimal education savings needed for schooling, though you may need savings for university or private tutoring.
  • Private education (within the Gulf): 30,000-80,000 SAR annually per child depending on school, city, and grade level. Total 540,000-1,440,000 SAR per child from KG to grade 12 (18 years). Requires a disciplined savings plan.
  • University abroad: 100,000-300,000 SAR annually (tuition + living expenses) depending on country and major. Total 400,000-1,200,000 SAR for four years. Requires early and intensive savings.
  • Online learning or local universities: 30,000-80,000 SAR annually for local private universities. A middle-ground option lower in cost than overseas study.

Many families choose a hybrid approach: private K-12 education with local university scholarships or government sponsorship for higher education. Discuss the available options with your family and plan accordingly.

FAQ: Education Savings

How much do I need to save monthly per child?
It depends on the type of education (public/private/overseas) and the child's current age. Roughly: for private K-12 education in the Gulf, 800-1,500 SAR per month from birth. For a university degree abroad, 1,500-3,000 SAR per month. Use the savings calculator for a precise figure.

Are education investment funds safe?
No investment is completely safe. Diversification across a balanced portfolio (equities + sukuk + real estate) reduces risk. As the child approaches university age (less than 5 years away), shift a larger portion to cash or savings accounts to protect capital.

What if my child doesn't want to attend university?
The savings can be used for any educational purpose (vocational training, professional certifications, starting a small business) or redirected to another child or even your retirement. Keep the flexibility.

Does the Gulf government provide education support?
Yes, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Qatar offer scholarship and sponsorship programs, but they are competitive and may not cover all fields or full costs. Do not rely on them alone , treat them as a supplement to your personal savings plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I start saving for my children's education?

The earlier you start, the more you benefit from compounding over time, even if the monthly amount is small at first.

How much should I save each month?

It depends on your target cost and the time remaining; start with a steady, sustainable amount and increase it gradually as income improves.

What saving options are available in the Gulf?

They include savings accounts, savings plans, investment funds and education insurance programs, each with a different level of risk and return.

How do I balance education saving with retirement?

Do not sacrifice your retirement entirely for education; set a realistic priority and balance both goals within a long-term plan.

Conclusion

Your children's education is the most expensive investment you will ever make, and the biggest mistake is delaying it. Compound interest is either your greatest ally (if you start early) or your worst enemy (if you delay). Set a target, choose suitable investment tools, automate your transfers, and protect the plan from lifestyle creep. Start today , even with a small amount , rather than waiting until the amount feels "comfortable." Use our savings-goal calculator to build your personalized plan.

Practical Tips for Saving for Education Gulf

When it comes to Saving for Education Gulf, Gulf families can benefit greatly from following a structured approach that takes into account their unique needs, cultural values, and lifestyle preferences. Start by identifying your family's specific requirements and priorities, then research available options thoroughly before making any decisions or commitments that could affect your family's budget, time, and overall well-being.

Planning ahead is essential for success. Create a clear roadmap that outlines your goals, timeline, and resources needed to achieve the best possible outcomes for your family. Consider factors such as seasonal changes, school schedules, work commitments, and religious observances that may impact your plans and choices throughout the year.

Involving all family members in the decision-making process from an early stage helps ensure everyone feels valued and invested in the outcome. Hold regular family discussions to share ideas, address concerns, and reach consensus on important matters. This collaborative approach not only leads to better decisions but also strengthens family bonds and teaches children valuable skills in communication, negotiation, and compromise that will benefit them throughout their lives.
