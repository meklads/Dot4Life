End of Service Benefits for Expats in Saudi Arabia and UAE: Complete Guide

June 8, 2026·15 min read·Expat Benefits

Introduction: Your Largest Single Payout

For most expats working in Saudi Arabia or the UAE, the end-of-service (EOS) benefit is the single largest lump sum payment they will ever receive from their employer. Yet many expats do not fully understand how it is calculated, what their legal rights are, or how to make the most of this money when they receive it. Whether you plan to stay in the Gulf for a few more years or are preparing to return home, understanding your EOS benefit is essential for your financial planning.

Section 1: End-of-Service Benefits in Saudi Arabia

Under Saudi Labor Law, employees are entitled to an end-of-service benefit upon termination of employment (except for termination for cause). The calculation is based on your final basic salary (excluding housing, transportation, and other allowances). For the first 5 years of service, you receive half a month's salary per year. For each year beyond 5 years, you receive one full month's salary per year. Example: 10 years at 10,000 SAR basic monthly salary. First 5 years: 5 x (10,000 / 2) = 25,000 SAR. Next 5 years: 5 x 10,000 = 50,000 SAR. Total: 75,000 SAR. If you resign, the benefit is reduced: 2/3 of the full amount after 2-5 years, 2/3 after 5-10 years, and the full amount after 10+ years.

Section 2: End-of-Service Benefits in the UAE

Under UAE Labor Law, gratuity is calculated based on basic salary. For employees with 1-5 years of service: 21 days of basic salary per year. For employees with 5+ years: 30 days of basic salary per year. The total is capped at two years of salary. Example: 10 years at 10,000 AED basic salary. Daily wage: 10,000 / 30 = 333 AED. First 5 years: 5 x 21 x 333 = 34,965 AED. Next 5 years: 5 x 30 x 333 = 49,950 AED. Total: 84,915 AED. For resignations, the reduction is: full entitlement after 3+ years for unlimited contracts, and after 5+ years for limited contracts.

Section 3: Key Legal Considerations

Understanding your legal rights protects you from being underpaid. Key points: the EOS benefit is calculated on basic salary only, not total package (this is why many employers keep basic salary low and allowances high); if you are terminated for cause (gross misconduct), you may lose your EOS entitlement; if you resign, the benefit is reduced (except after long service); the benefit must be paid within 2 weeks of termination in Saudi Arabia and within 14 days in the UAE; if your employer refuses to pay, you can file a complaint with the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development in Saudi Arabia or the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation in the UAE.

Section 4: Strategies to Maximize Your EOS Benefit

While you cannot directly control how your employer calculates EOS, you can take steps to maximize it: negotiate a higher basic salary during job offers (since allowances do not count toward EOS), understand your contract type (limited vs unlimited in UAE affects resignation entitlement), plan your departure timing (staying past the 5-year mark in Saudi Arabia significantly increases the benefit rate), and avoid termination for cause at all costs (you lose the entire benefit).

Section 5: What to Do With Your EOS Payment

When you receive your EOS payout, resist the temptation to spend it all at once. This is likely the largest lump sum you will receive, and how you use it will significantly impact your financial future. Priorities: pay off high-interest debt (credit cards, personal loans), fund your emergency fund (3-6 months of expenses in your home country), invest for retirement (especially for expats without pension systems), fund children's education (if returning home, school fees may be different), and then allocate to other goals (home down payment, business investment, etc.). Section 6: Why You Should Not Rely Only on EOS

Many expats make the mistake of viewing their EOS benefit as their only retirement or savings plan. This is dangerous for several reasons: the amount is often less than expected (basic salary is typically only 50-70% of total package), you may leave before accumulating a significant benefit, the benefit is not adjusted for inflation, you have no control over when or how it is paid, and there is always a risk your employer may not pay (though legal recourse exists). The solution: build your own savings and investment plan alongside your EOS benefit. Save and invest a portion of your salary each month as if the EOS benefit did not exist. Then when you receive your EOS, it becomes a bonus that accelerates your financial goals.

Action Plan

Step 1: Calculate your current EOS entitlement using the formulas above.
Step 2: Open a separate investment account and automate monthly transfers equal to 10% of your basic salary.
Step 3: Review your EOS calculation annually and ensure your employer is recording it correctly.
Step 4: Create a plan for your EOS payout before you receive it, including debt payoff, emergency fund, and investment allocation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens to my EOS benefit if I change jobs within the Gulf?

When you leave one employer, they pay your EOS benefit. Your new employer starts fresh. There is no transfer of EOS between employers in either country. This is why it is important to negotiate your starting basic salary with each new employer.

Is EOS benefit taxable in Saudi Arabia or the UAE?

No. There is no personal income tax in either country, and EOS benefits are not subject to tax. However, if you are a US citizen, you may need to report it on your US tax return.

Can my employer deduct from my EOS benefit?

Deductions are only permitted for specific reasons defined in the labor law, such as outstanding loans from the employer or damage caused by the employee. Your employer cannot arbitrarily deduct from your EOS benefit.

Conclusion

Your end-of-service benefit is a significant financial asset, but it should not be your only retirement or savings plan. Understand how it is calculated, know your legal rights, build your own independent savings alongside it, and have a clear plan for how you will use the money when it arrives. With proper planning, your EOS benefit can be a powerful tool to accelerate your family's financial goals.
