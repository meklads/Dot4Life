Al-Masjid an-Nabawi: The Heart of Medina

Al-Masjid an-Nabawi (The Prophet's Mosque) is the second holiest mosque in Islam after Masjid al-Haram in Makkah. It was built by Prophet Muhammad ﷺ himself when he migrated to Medina, and it is his final resting place. The mosque holds an unparalleled place in the hearts of Muslims worldwide, serving as a spiritual destination for pilgrims, Umrah performers, and visitors from every corner of the globe.

The mosque welcomes millions of visitors annually, especially during Hajj, Umrah seasons, and Ramadan. This comprehensive guide will take you through everything you need to know about Masjid an-Nabawi — from its rich history to its most significant landmarks, from proper visitation etiquette to the best nearby hotels and practical tips for an unforgettable spiritual journey.

The History of Masjid an-Nabawi

When Prophet Muhammad ﷺ arrived in Medina during the Hijrah (migration) from Makkah, building a mosque was his first priority. The Prophet ﷺ chose a location where his camel sat down, on land owned by two orphans, Sahl and Suhayl. He purchased the land and constructed the mosque using sun-dried bricks (adobe), palm trunks, and stones.

The original mosque measured approximately 35 by 30 meters, with a height of no more than two arm's lengths. The construction took several months, with the companions (Sahabah) actively participating alongside the Prophet ﷺ, reciting: "O Allah, there is no good except the good of the Hereafter, so forgive the Ansar and the Muhajirun."

Over the centuries, Masjid an-Nabawi underwent numerous expansions — from the era of the Rightly Guided Caliphs, through the Umayyad, Abbasid, and Ottoman periods, to the modern Saudi expansions that have increased its capacity to over 1.6 million worshippers.

Capacity and Architecture

Today, Masjid an-Nabawi covers approximately 400,500 square meters and can accommodate over 1.6 million worshippers during peak times. The Grand Saudi Expansion, ordered by the Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques, added vast areas including shaded courtyards equipped with giant umbrellas that provide comfort during the hot Medina summers.

The mosque features multiple prayer halls, interconnected arcades, and state-of-the-art cooling and ventilation systems, ensuring a comfortable experience for all visitors year-round.

Key Landmarks Inside the Mosque

1. The Rawdah (The Noble Garden)

The Rawdah is the most sacred area within Masjid an-Nabawi, located between the Prophet's pulpit (minbar) and his house (where he is buried). The Prophet ﷺ said: "What is between my house and my pulpit is a garden from the gardens of Paradise." The Rawdah covers approximately 330 square meters and is distinguished by its green carpet, contrasting with the rest of the mosque's red carpet.

Entry to the Rawdah requires an electronic permit obtained through the "Tawakkalna" or "Eatmarna" apps. Specific times are allocated for men and women. Early booking is recommended, especially during busy seasons, as capacity is limited.

2. The Noble Grave (Rawdah Mubarak)

The grave of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is located in the chamber of Aisha (RA), which was originally adjacent to the mosque. Beside him lie Abu Bakr al-Siddiq and Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA). Visitors stand before the grave and offer greetings of peace (salaam) with respect and reverence.

The recommended greeting is: "Peace be upon you, O Messenger of Allah, and the mercy of Allah and His blessings," followed by greetings to Abu Bakr and Umar.

3. The Minbar (Pulpit)

The minbar of Masjid an-Nabawi is the pulpit from which the Prophet ﷺ delivered his sermons. The current minbar is not the original one, though it stands in the same location. The original minbar was made from tamarisk wood.

4. The Mihrab

The mihrab indicates the direction of prayer (qibla) and marks the spot where the Prophet ﷺ led prayers. There are two notable mihrabs within the mosque: the old Prophetic mihrab and the Ottoman mihrab.

5. Famous Pillars (Aswatin)

Several historic pillars inside the mosque each have a unique story. Key pillars include:

  • Pillar of al-Haras: Where the Prophet's guards sat
  • Pillar of al-Wufud: Where the Prophet received delegations
  • Pillar of al-Mahrath: Associated with the revelation of the Quran
  • Pillar of al-Tawbah: Where Abu Lubaba tied himself in repentance
  • Pillar of Aisha: Where the Prophet prayed at night

Prayer Times at Masjid an-Nabawi

The five daily prayers are held at their prescribed times. Prayer times vary by season and can be checked through official apps or the mosque's website. Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, and Isha prayers are performed with large congregations, and Taraweeh prayers during Ramadan draw especially large crowds.

Visiting Masjid an-Nabawi: Practical Tips

Best Times to Visit

The mosque can be visited year-round, but the best times are outside peak seasons to avoid crowds. The period from Muharram to Rajab is ideal, as is the period immediately after Hajj season.

Dress Code

Modest clothing is required. Men should wear loose-fitting, modest attire — preferably white. Women must wear a full abaya covering the body, with hair covered.

Etiquette

  • Enter with the right foot, reciting the mosque entry supplication
  • Pray two rak'ahs as a greeting to the mosque (Tahiyyat al-Masjid)
  • Greet the Prophet ﷺ with respect and lowered voice
  • Avoid raising voices or disturbing worshippers
  • Refrain from photography during prayer times
  • Maintain cleanliness and respect the sanctity of the place

Hotels Near Masjid an-Nabawi

Multiple hotels near Masjid an-Nabawi cater to all budgets. Five-star options include the Marriott, Sheraton, Mercure, and Mövenpick — all within walking distance. Four-star hotels like Dar al-Taqwa, Dar al-Iman, and Qubbat al-Madinah offer excellent value. Budget hotels in the central area provide affordable options 5-10 minutes away on foot.

Restaurants Near the Mosque

Many restaurants and cafés around the mosque serve local and international cuisine. Popular options include Al-Anab for traditional dishes, Mazak al-Madinah for Arabic food, and Madinah Coffee for dates and Arabic coffee.

Transportation

Reaching Masjid an-Nabawi is convenient via Prince Mohammed bin Abdulaziz International Airport (20 minutes by car), the Haramain High-Speed Railway connecting Makkah and Medina, and taxis and buses available throughout the city.

The Status of Masjid an-Nabawi in Quran and Sunnah

Masjid an-Nabawi holds an exalted status in Islam. Allah mentions in Surah at-Tawbah (9:108): "Indeed, a mosque founded on righteousness from the first day is more worthy for you to stand in. Within it are men who love to purify themselves; and Allah loves those who purify themselves." Prayer in the Prophet's Mosque carries immense reward; the Prophet ﷺ said: "One prayer in this mosque of mine is better than a thousand prayers elsewhere, except in al-Masjid al-Haram."

The mosque is one of only three to which travel is recommended for worship, as the Prophet ﷺ said: "Do not set out on a journey except to three mosques: al-Masjid al-Haram, this mosque of mine, and al-Masjid al-Aqsa." This makes it a primary destination for Muslims worldwide — whether pilgrims, Umrah performers, or visitors.

Historical Expansions of Masjid an-Nabawi

Masjid an-Nabawi has undergone numerous major expansions throughout history, each adding new areas and improving facilities:

First Expansion under the Rightly Guided Caliphs: During Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab's reign (17 AH / 638 CE), the mosque was first expanded from 1,400 to 3,600 square meters. Later, during Uthman ibn Affan's reign (29 AH / 650 CE), another expansion added stone walls and columns, increasing the area to 4,500 square meters.

Umayyad Expansion: Caliph al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik ordered a comprehensive expansion supervised by Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz. This included building the first recessed mihrab in Islamic history and incorporating Aisha's chamber (containing the Prophet's grave) into the mosque.

Abbasid Expansion: Caliph al-Mahdi expanded the mosque northward and renovated its walls and ceilings. The Green Dome, which has become a distinctive symbol of Masjid an-Nabawi, was built during this period over the Prophet's chamber.

Ottoman Expansion: Sultan Abdul Majid I (1265 AH / 1849 CE) undertook a major renovation and expansion. Wooden columns were replaced with stone ones, the Green Dome was restored, and distinctive Islamic ornamentation was added — much of which remains visible today.

Grand Saudi Expansions: Beginning with King Abdulaziz Al Saud, successive Saudi expansions continued through the reigns of Kings Fahd, Abdullah, and the latest major expansion under King Salman bin Abdulaziz. The most recent expansion added vast shaded courtyards equipped with giant umbrellas that open and close automatically, elevated walkways, escalators, cutting-edge cooling and ventilation systems, and advanced sound and lighting technology.

The Prophetic Library and Museums

Masjid an-Nabawi houses a historic library containing thousands of rare manuscripts and books on Islamic sciences. Some manuscripts date back to the early Islamic centuries. Museums within the mosque complex showcase its history, construction phases, and evolution through the ages, including rare models and photographs.

Services for Visitors

  • Free electric carts: For the elderly and people with special needs
  • Guidance centers: Information desks for visitor questions
  • Digital library: Thousands of Islamic resources for free download
  • Smart apps: Official apps providing prayer times, visiting schedules, and interactive maps
  • Health centers: Free medical clinics equipped for emergencies
  • Zamzam water stations: Chilled Zamzam water available throughout the mosque
  • Ablution facilities: Clean, well-maintained facilities at all entrances

Medina: The Spiritual Destination

Medina was the first capital in Islamic history and the destination of the Prophet's Hijrah. The city has a unique spiritual atmosphere. Key landmarks besides Masjid an-Nabawi include Quba Mosque (the first mosque in Islam), Al-Baqi Cemetery, Mount Uhud, the Mountain of Archers, Masjid al-Qiblatain, and historic wells such as Bir Rumah and Bir Uthman.

Medina balances its religious heritage with modern development, offering luxury hotels, shopping centers, and diverse dining options. Transportation is convenient via taxis, buses, and ride-hailing apps. The region is also known for its agricultural products, particularly dates such as Ajwa, Anbari, and Ruthana — recommended as souvenirs from local markets.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit to enter Masjid an-Nabawi?

Entry for prayer does not require a permit. However, visiting the Rawdah to greet the Prophet ﷺ requires an electronic permit via Tawakkalna or Eatmarna apps.

What are Rawdah visiting hours?

Visits are available in two shifts: morning (after Fajr until Dhuhr) and evening (after Isha until Fajr). Times vary by season.

Can women visit Masjid an-Nabawi?

Yes, women have a dedicated prayer area. Women can also visit the Rawdah during allocated times.
