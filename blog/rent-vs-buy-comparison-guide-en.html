Rent vs Buy: Comparison Guide

2026-06-088 min read

Abu Abdullah's Story: Renting Cost Him More Than Buying

"I always believed renting was cheaper than buying, but after 7 years I realized I lost more than 300,000 SAR." That is how Abu Abdullah, a 42-year-old father of four from Jeddah, describes his experience with the real estate market. He was renting an apartment in the Al-Nahda district of Jeddah at an annual rent of 42,000 SAR. Over 7 years, he paid 294,000 SAR in rent without owning any asset. In contrast, he could have purchased a villa in Al-Wadi district for 800,000 SAR through a real estate loan from the Social Development Bank with a subsidized interest rate of just 3 percent, paying a monthly installment of 3,500 SAR, which is less than his current rent. "If I had bought from the beginning, the villa would be mine now, or at least 60 percent of it after 7 years of installments, instead of 294,000 SAR gone to the landlord. The difference between renting and owning over this period exceeds 500,000 SAR when accounting for the property's appreciation." But he adds cautiously: "Buying is not always the better option. My colleague at work bought a villa in another city and then had to move due to his job transfer. He sold it at a 15 percent loss just two years after purchase. The loss was greater than if he had rented throughout." Abu Abdullah's story represents a dilemma faced by thousands of Gulf families every day: rent versus buy. The decision is not simple and depends on several personal and financial factors that this comparative guide will examine in detail to help you make the right choice for your specific situation.

Rent vs Buy: A Comprehensive Overview

The rent versus buy decision is one of the most important financial decisions a family makes, and its impact extends for decades. In Saudi Arabia, the average villa price reached 1.2 million SAR in Riyadh and 1.1 million SAR in Jeddah in 2025, while the average annual apartment rent ranges between 30,000 and 60,000 SAR depending on the neighborhood and size. In the United Arab Emirates, the average annual rent for a two-bedroom apartment in Dubai is approximately 80,000 to 120,000 AED, while the purchase price for a similar apartment starts from 500,000 AED. These figures vary significantly between luxury neighborhoods and more affordable areas. The Saudi Real Estate Authority provides regular market reports and price indices through its official website, which can help you assess market conditions in your specific area. The key factors that determine which option is better include: expected length of stay in the location, job stability, ability to save for a down payment, mortgage interest rates, and future real estate market expectations in your area. Each of these factors must be carefully evaluated because the wrong choice could cost you hundreds of thousands of dirhams or riyals over the long term.

Advantages of Buying: Why Many Prefer Ownership

Buying a home offers significant financial and psychological advantages. First, long-term wealth building: every installment you pay increases your equity in the property, and when the loan ends, you own the asset outright, typically at a value higher than the purchase price. Second, stability: home ownership means no worry about annual rent increases or eviction when the lease ends. Third, freedom to customize: you can renovate and modify your home as you wish without needing landlord approval. Fourth, inflation hedge: real estate historically appreciates at rates that exceed inflation in most Gulf markets, protecting your wealth's purchasing power. Fifth, rental income potential: if you buy a larger property than you need, you can rent out part of it to cover a portion of the monthly loan payment. The Sakani platform, part of the Ministry of Housing, offers subsidized financing solutions for citizens through its official portal, including real estate loans of up to 500,000 SAR with low interest rates for qualified applicants.

Advantages of Renting: Flexibility Above All

Renting is not a bad option in many cases, and may actually be better than buying under certain circumstances. First, flexibility: you can move easily when your job changes or your family needs change, without bearing the costs of selling and buying. Second, lower upfront costs: renting does not require a large down payment (typically 20 to 30 percent of the property value), freeing your savings for investment in other areas such as stocks or business ventures. Third, no maintenance costs: the landlord is responsible for major maintenance and structural repairs, which can save you 1 to 2 percent of the property value annually. Fourth, investment diversification: instead of concentrating your wealth in a single property, you can distribute your investments across different asset classes. Fifth, avoiding market volatility: the real estate market can decline, and you may find yourself paying a mortgage on a property worth less than its purchase price. The Saudi Central Bank publishes real estate price indices and mortgage financing data on its official website, helping borrowers make informed decisions about market timing and affordability.

The 5-Year Rule: When to Buy and When to Rent

Financial advisors recommend a simple but powerful rule: if you plan to stay in the location for more than 5 years, buying is usually better financially. If you will leave before that, renting is the wiser choice. This rule is based on the fact that the costs of buying and selling a property, including real estate agent commissions (2 to 3 percent), registration fees (approximately 1 percent), appraisal fees, and loan origination fees, total between 5 and 10 percent of the property value. It takes several years of expected property appreciation to recover these costs. For example, if you buy a villa for 1 million SAR, you will pay approximately 50,000 to 80,000 SAR in purchase costs and closing fees. If you sell after only two years, and the market is stable or declining, you could lose a significant portion of these costs. In contrast, renting for two years does not incur additional costs when you move. For this reason, career and family stability is the critical factor. If you are in the early stages of your career, work in a field that requires moving between cities such as consulting or contracting, or are uncertain about your long-term plans, renting is the more prudent option regardless of how attractive property prices may seem. However, if you have a stable government job or secure private sector position with a family, buying is the better long-term investment.

Mortgage Financing in Saudi Arabia: Available Options

Real estate financing in Saudi Arabia has developed significantly in recent years thanks to the Housing Program initiatives and Vision 2030. Banks offer real estate financing of up to 90 percent of the property value for citizens through the subsidized mortgage program from the Ministry of Housing, meaning citizens only need a down payment of 10 to 20 percent. Average interest rates on real estate loans in Saudi Arabia range from 3 to 5 percent annually for subsidized loans from the Real Estate Development Fund, and 5 to 7 percent for conventional loans from commercial banks. Loan terms extend up to 30 years, which reduces the monthly installment and makes it affordable for middle-income families. Monthly installments must not exceed 30 percent of the borrower's monthly income according to Saudi Central Bank regulations, ensuring borrowers are not overburdened. For non-citizens, financing options are more limited and require a larger down payment (typically 30 to 50 percent), higher interest rates (6 to 9 percent), and shorter terms (15 to 20 years). Terms also vary by bank and depend on residency type and profession. Some banks require a minimum monthly income between 15,000 and 25,000 SAR for expatriate applicants. Frequently Asked Questions

Is renting a waste of money compared to buying?

Not always. Renting offers flexibility, lower upfront costs, and the freedom to invest in other areas. Buying is better when you plan to stay long-term and the market is stable. The golden rule: if you will stay more than 5 years, buying is usually better. If less, renting is often the superior choice.

How much money do I need to buy a home in Saudi Arabia?

You need a down payment of 20 to 30 percent of the property value. For an average-priced villa of 1.2 million SAR, this means 240,000 to 360,000 SAR. For qualified citizens, the Sakani program provides support of up to 150,000 SAR to help with these costs.

When is renting better than buying?

Renting is better when: you are in the early stages of your career and expect to move, you do not have enough savings for a down payment, you are uncertain about staying in the city, or when property prices are overvalued compared to rents in your area.

Can expats buy property in Saudi Arabia?

Yes, expats can buy property in specific areas according to the non-Saudi ownership system. Financing is available but with stricter conditions, requiring a larger down payment (30 to 50 percent) and higher interest rates. Consult a specialized real estate advisor about options for your specific situation.

What are the hidden costs of buying a home?

Purchase costs go beyond the property price. They include: registration fees (about 1 percent of property value), real estate agent commission (2 to 3 percent), property appraisal fees, loan origination fees, annual maintenance costs (1 to 2 percent of property value), and property insurance. Factor these into your budget before making a decision.

The rent versus buy decision is not purely financial but a life decision that depends on your personal, professional, and family circumstances. There is no universally correct option that suits everyone. What matters is making your decision based on accurate information and sound financial planning. Use the Mortgage Calculator to compare different scenarios and calculate the total cost of each option over 10 to 20 years. Remember that the goal is not to own property at any cost, but to achieve the financial and family stability that suits you and your family. Take your time, consult with professionals, and choose the path that aligns with your long-term goals.
