BMI for Middle Eastern Adults: What Is a Healthy BMI Really?

June 8, 2026·8 min read·Health & Body Composition

Introduction

In June 2023, the American Medical Association (AMA) made a landmark decision: Body Mass Index (BMI) is not accurate enough to be used as the sole measure of health. This decision was not surprising to researchers who have known for decades that BMI has serious limitations, especially for people of Middle Eastern, Asian, and African descent.

The problem is particularly acute for Middle Eastern populations. Studies consistently show that Arabs and Middle Easterners tend to accumulate visceral fat — the dangerous fat around internal organs — even when their BMI falls within the "normal" range. This means a person with a BMI of 23 could have higher health risks than someone with a BMI of 27, simply because of where their body stores fat.

Key Takeaway

For Middle Eastern adults, waist-to-height ratio (WHtR) is more accurate than BMI. The golden rule: keep your waist circumference less than half your height. If you are 170 cm tall, your waist should be under 85 cm.

Why BMI Fails Middle Eastern Populations

BMI = weight (kg) / height (m)². It has been the global standard since the 19th century, but its flaws are increasingly hard to ignore:

  • Does not distinguish muscle from fat: A muscular athlete and an obese person can have the same BMI.
  • Ignores fat distribution: Where fat is stored matters more than total body fat. Visceral belly fat is far more dangerous than subcutaneous fat.
  • Based on Western populations: The original BMI data came from European men. Body composition varies significantly across ethnicities.
  • Ethnic bias: At the same BMI, Middle Easterners have higher body fat percentage and more visceral fat than Caucasians.

A landmark 2022 study published in The Lancet examined over 100,000 participants across 15 countries and found that Middle Eastern populations had significantly higher cardiometabolic risk at every BMI level compared to European populations.

What Research Says About Middle Eastern Body Composition

Multiple studies have documented the "normal-weight obesity" phenomenon among Arabs. A 2023 study in the Journal of Epidemiology and Global Health found that nearly 40% of Saudi adults with a "normal" BMI (under 25) had excess body fat percentage and elevated metabolic risk factors.

This happens because Middle Eastern populations tend to:

  • Accumulate more visceral fat at lower BMI levels
  • Have higher body fat percentage overall at the same BMI
  • Experience metabolic complications at lower BMIs (diabetes, hypertension starting at BMI 23-24)

Waist-to-Height Ratio: The Gold Standard

Waist-to-height ratio (WHtR) has emerged as the most practical and accurate metric for assessing health risk, particularly for Middle Eastern populations. Dr. Margaret Ashwell, who pioneered WHtR research, demonstrated that it predicts cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes more accurately than BMI.

Here is how to calculate and interpret it:

  1. Measure your waist at the level of your navel, breathing normally.
  2. Measure your height without shoes.
  3. Divide waist (cm) by height (cm).
  4. Interpret: Below 0.5 = Healthy | 0.5 to 0.6 = Moderate risk | Above 0.6 = High risk

Example: Waist 90 cm, Height 170 cm → 90 ÷ 170 = 0.53. This person has moderate risk and should focus on reducing belly fat.

Combining BMI with Other Metrics

The most comprehensive approach uses BMI as a starting point, then adds waist measurement and body composition analysis:

  • BMI under 25 + WHtR under 0.5: Excellent — maintain current habits.
  • BMI under 25 + WHtR over 0.5: "Normal weight obesity" — focus on nutrition and exercise to reduce visceral fat despite normal weight.
  • BMI 25-30 + WHtR under 0.5: Possibly muscular build — check body fat percentage for clarity.
  • BMI 25-30 + WHtR over 0.5: Increased risk — consult a healthcare provider.
  • BMI over 30 + WHtR over 0.6: High risk — medical consultation strongly recommended.

Practical Steps for Better Health

Regardless of your numbers, these evidence-based strategies work for everyone:

  1. Reduce added sugars and refined carbohydrates: These directly contribute to visceral fat accumulation common in Middle Eastern diets.
  2. Increase protein and fiber: They improve satiety and support muscle maintenance during weight loss.
  3. Walk 30 minutes daily: Even moderate walking significantly reduces visceral fat.
  4. Add resistance training twice weekly: Building muscle improves your body composition regardless of the scale.
  5. Sleep 7-8 hours: Poor sleep is directly linked to increased belly fat storage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is BMI accurate for Middle Eastern populations?
No. Research consistently shows that BMI underestimates health risks for Middle Easterners because it does not account for visceral fat accumulation common in this population at normal BMI levels.

What is a healthy BMI for Middle Eastern adults?
While standard BMI categories apply (18.5-24.9 is normal), Middle Eastern adults should prioritize waist-to-height ratio. Keeping your waist below half your height is more predictive of health outcomes than BMI alone.

What is waist-to-height ratio?
It is your waist circumference divided by your height. A result below 0.5 is healthy. This metric predicts cardiovascular risk better than BMI for Middle Eastern populations.

Why do Middle Easterners have more visceral fat?
Genetic predisposition, dietary patterns high in refined carbohydrates, and lower physical activity levels all contribute to higher visceral fat accumulation.

How can I measure body composition at home?
Start with our BMI calculator, then measure your waist circumference at navel level. Divide by your height. For a more complete picture, use a body fat scale or skinfold calipers.

What BMI is considered overweight for Middle Easterners?
Some researchers suggest lowering the overweight threshold to BMI 23 for Middle Eastern populations, similar to Asian guidelines, due to increased metabolic risk at lower BMIs.

Conclusion

BMI is not your enemy, but it is not the complete truth either. For Middle Eastern adults, relying solely on BMI can give a false sense of security. The combination of BMI, waist-to-height ratio, and body composition analysis provides a much more accurate picture of your health. Use our BMI calculator and body fat calculator as starting points, then focus on sustainable lifestyle changes that improve your actual health rather than just a number on the scale.
