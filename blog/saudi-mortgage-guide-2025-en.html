Best Home Loans in Saudi Arabia 2025, Bank Comparison Guide

8 min read

Introduction

Buying your first home is a life-changing decision, but choosing the wrong financing could cost you tens of thousands in extra payments over the life of your loan. In Saudi Arabia, home financing options range from conventional to Islamic banks, with substantial government support available through the Real Estate Development Fund (REDF). Navigating these options without proper understanding can lead to costly mistakes.

Before you visit any bank or sign any contract, you need to understand the fundamentals of mortgage financing in Saudi Arabia: the types available, the requirements, the best banks, and how REDF subsidies affect your monthly payments. In this comprehensive guide, we provide an objective comparison of the top 5 banks for home financing in Saudi Arabia 2025, along with a complete breakdown of requirements and common first-time buyer mistakes.

Quick Summary

Al Ahli Bank offers the highest financing amounts (up to SAR 6 million). Al Rajhi Bank is best for Islamic Murabaha. Alinma Bank offers the fastest approvals. REDF provides up to SAR 500,000 in subsidies. Your monthly payment should not exceed 33% of your salary.

Mortgage Requirements in Saudi Arabia

Before applying for any home financing, make sure you meet the basic requirements that Saudi banks typically demand. These requirements are largely standardized across banks, with minor variations.

Minimum Salary: Most banks require a minimum salary of SAR 4,000 for employees and SAR 5,000 for self-employed applicants. Some banks may require higher thresholds for larger financing amounts.

Maximum Installment Ratio (33%): Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) regulations require that your monthly payment does not exceed 33% of your monthly salary. This includes all existing financial obligations. If you have other commitments (car loan, credit cards), your available capacity will be lower.

SIMAH Credit Record: Any previous defaults or late payments may result in your application being rejected. Check your SIMAH credit report before applying, and make sure it's error-free. A minimum clearance period of 6 months is typically required after any default.

Down Payment: Banks typically require a down payment of 10-25% of the property value. With REDF support, the down payment can be as low as 10%. Without support, you may need 25% or more.

Employment History: Banks typically require proof of stable income for at least 6 months. New employees may need a longer period or qualify for a lower financing amount.

REDF Subsidy Program

The Real Estate Development Fund (REDF) is a Saudi government initiative aimed at helping Saudi citizens own their first home. The fund provides a subsidy of up to SAR 500,000, distributed as support for monthly installments rather than as a cash payment.

Who qualifies? Saudi citizens purchasing their first home, who have not previously received government housing support. Eligibility depends on monthly income, marital status, and number of dependents. Priority is given to the most needy families.

How to apply? Register on the official REDF website (redf.gov.sa). After registration, your eligibility will be assessed and you will be notified of your subsidy amount. You can then visit any participating bank with your eligibility certificate.

How does it affect your monthly payment? The subsidy reduces your monthly payment. For example, if your original monthly payment is SAR 4,000, the subsidy may cover a portion of it, significantly lowering what you actually pay. The amount varies based on your eligibility grade.

Practical Example: Calculate Your Monthly Payment

Let's take a practical example of a Saudi employee earning SAR 15,000 who wants to buy an apartment worth SAR 800,000:

Property value SAR 800,000
Down payment (10%) SAR 80,000
Financing amount SAR 720,000
Financing term 20 years (240 months)
Approximate monthly payment SAR 4,200
Debt-to-income ratio 28% (within limit)

In this example, the debt-to-income ratio of 28% is below the maximum of 33%, meaning the application is acceptable from a debt ratio standpoint. However, other financial obligations must also be considered.

Calculation steps:
1. Determine the property value you want to purchase.
2. Calculate the down payment (10-25% depending on your situation).
3. Subtract the down payment from the property value = financing amount.
4. Use a mortgage calculator to get your monthly payment.
5. Verify that the payment does not exceed 33% of your monthly salary.
6. Add REDF subsidy if eligible, your effective payment will decrease.

5 Mistakes First-Time Buyers Make

1. Applying to only one bank: Each bank has different offers and rates. Applying to multiple banks gives you better options. You can use a mortgage broker to compare offers on your behalf.

2. Not checking SIMAH before applying: Some applicants discover defaults only after their application is rejected. Get your SIMAH report beforehand and ensure it's error-free.

3. Choosing the longest term without calculating total cost: A 30-year term reduces monthly payments but significantly increases the total cost of financing. Choose the shortest term you can afford.

4. Forgetting registration and transfer fees: Municipal registration fees, Ministry of Justice transfer fees, and property valuation fees can add SAR 10,000-20,000 in additional costs.

5. Not applying for REDF subsidy: Thousands of eligible citizens don't apply for the subsidy. Even if you're unsure about your eligibility, submit your application, the process is free and straightforward.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which bank offers the best mortgage in Saudi Arabia 2025?
The best bank depends on your situation. Al Ahli Bank offers the highest financing amounts, Al Rajhi Bank provides excellent Islamic Murabaha products, and Alinma Bank is known for fast approvals. Compare offers before applying.

What are the requirements for a mortgage in Saudi Arabia?
Key requirements: minimum salary of SAR 4,000, age between 21-60, monthly payment not exceeding 33% of salary, stable employment for at least 6 months, and a clean SIMAH credit record.

What percentage of the property value can I finance?
Banks typically finance 90% for Saudi citizens with REDF support, or 75-85% without support. You must provide the down payment from your own savings.

What is the REDF subsidy in Saudi Arabia?
The Real Estate Development Fund (REDF) provides eligible Saudi citizens with a subsidy up to SAR 500,000 on their first home purchase. Eligibility depends on income, family status, and number of dependents.

What is the difference between Murabaha and Ijara financing?
In Murabaha, the bank buys the property and sells it to you at a higher price in installments. In Ijara (lease-to-own), you rent the property from the bank with the right to own it at the end. Both are Shariah-compliant.

Practical Decision Framework

When evaluating any financial decision, use this 3-step filter:
1. Necessity: Is this essential for your family's wellbeing in the Gulf context? Housing, education, and healthcare are non-negotiable. Luxury goods and premium services are not.
2. Sustainability: Can you sustain this expense for 12+ months without stress? If a mortgage payment requires more than 40% of your monthly net income, it is a financial risk.
3. Sharia Alignment: Does this decision comply with Islamic financial principles? Avoid interest-based debt, ensure Zakat obligations are calculated correctly, and consider the ethical impact of your investments.

Common mistake: Many Gulf families underestimate the cumulative cost of lifestyle expenses - daily coffee, dining out, premium school fees, and multiple car payments. These "small" expenses often exceed housing costs in total.

Conclusion

Before you visit the bank, calculate your monthly payment first. Mortgage financing is a long-term decision, and any mistake in choosing could cost you hundreds of thousands over the repayment period. Start by comparing bank offers, check your REDF eligibility, and accurately calculate your monthly payment before committing to any contract.

Remember that buying a home isn't just a financial transaction, it's an investment in your family's future. Choose financing that fits your income, not the one that offers you the largest amount. Long-term financial peace of mind is more important than getting your dream home at any cost.
