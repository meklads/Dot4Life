How to Calculate Zakat Correctly on Modern Investments and Assets

June 8, 2026·8 min read·Islamic

Introduction

Zakat is one of the five pillars of Islam, an act of worship and a means of purifying wealth. For many Muslims today, calculating Zakat has become more complex than ever. Beyond cash and gold, modern wealth includes stocks, retirement accounts, real estate investments, business assets, and even cryptocurrency.

Getting your Zakat calculation right is essential. Underpaying leaves an obligation unfulfilled; overpaying is unnecessary. This guide explains how to calculate Zakat on every major asset type in clear, practical terms.

The Basic Rule

Zakat is due at 2.5% of your Zakatable wealth if it exceeds the nisab (threshold) and has been in your possession for one lunar year (hawl). The nisab is approximately the value of 85 grams of gold or 595 grams of silver.

Step 1: Determine Your Zakat Date

Choose a consistent date each (lunar) year to calculate and pay Zakat. Many people use the first day of Ramadan. On that date, assess all your assets:

  • If your total Zakatable wealth exceeds the nisab, Zakat is due on the full amount.
  • If it is below the nisab, no Zakat is due until the next assessment.

Cash and Bank Accounts

This is the simplest category. Include:

  • Cash on hand
  • Checking and savings account balances
  • Money in current accounts, savings accounts, and investment accounts

Calculate Zakat at 2.5% of the total balance on your Zakat date. Do not deduct upcoming bills or expenses.

Gold and Silver

Gold and silver are Zakatable regardless of whether they are held as investments, jewelry, or bullion. The majority of scholars rule that jewelry (including gold worn by women) is subject to Zakat if it reaches the nisab. Calculate:

  • Weigh your gold and silver
  • Determine their current market value
  • If the total value exceeds the nisab, pay 2.5%

Stocks and Shares

Different types of stock investments have different Zakat rules:

  • Individual stocks: For most scholars, Zakat is due on the market value of shares held as investments. If you are a long-term holder, pay 2.5% of the market value. Some scholars calculate based on the company's Zakatable assets divided by the share price.
  • Growth stocks (no dividends): Still subject to Zakat on their market value. Growth is considered potential wealth.
  • Trading stocks (short-term): Zakat is due on the full market value at 2.5%.
  • Halal stock funds: Same as individual stocks. Pay 2.5% on the fund value on your Zakat date.

Real Estate

Real estate Zakat depends on the purpose of the investment:

  • Primary residence: Not Zakatable. Your home is not considered wealth subject to Zakat.
  • Rental properties: Zakat is due on the rental income received (if it meets the nisab and hawl), not on the property value itself. If you hold cash from rent, that cash is Zakatable.
  • Real estate held for sale (investment/flipping): Zakat is due on the market value of the property at 2.5% yearly.
  • Land held for future development: The majority view is that it is not Zakatable unless it is intended for sale.

Retirement Accounts and Pensions

Retirement accounts like IRA, 401(k), or similar plans:

  • Accessible retirement accounts: If you can withdraw (even with penalty), Zakat is due on the balance.
  • Government pensions: Not Zakatable until received. Once you receive the monthly payment, any amount remaining at your next Zakat date is Zakatable.
  • Employer-matched accounts: The vested portion (the amount you own) is Zakatable. The unvested portion is not yet your wealth.

Cryptocurrency

Cryptocurrency is a debated area, but the majority of contemporary scholars rule that:

  • If cryptocurrency is held as a medium of exchange (like cash), Zakat is due on its market value.
  • If it is held as an investment (like stocks), Zakat is due on the market value at 2.5%.
  • The nisab and hawl conditions still apply.
  • Use the market value on your Zakat date in your local currency.

Business Assets and Inventory

For business owners:

  • Inventory (goods for sale): Zakatable at 2.5% of the market value.
  • Accounts receivable: Money owed to you by reliable debtors is Zakatable. Debts from unreliable sources may not be.
  • Business equipment and machinery: Not Zakatable according to the majority of scholars. Only goods intended for sale.
  • Cash in the business bank account: Fully Zakatable.

What Is NOT Zakatable

  • Your primary residence and its furnishings
  • Personal vehicles
  • Clothing, food, and household items
  • Business equipment and machinery (not for sale)
  • Debts owed to you that are unlikely to be recovered
  • Your personal tools or professional equipment

Putting It All Together: A Simple Method

The easiest way is to use our Zakat calculator, but here is a manual approach:

  1. Add up all Zakatable assets (cash, gold, stocks, investment properties for sale, crypto, business inventory, accounts receivable)
  2. Subtract your immediate liabilities (credit card debt, loans due within the year, unpaid bills)
  3. If the remainder exceeds the nisab, pay 2.5% of the total

Conclusion

Zakat is a beautiful act of worship that purifies your wealth and supports the community. Modern wealth has many forms, but the underlying principle remains the same: 2.5% of your accumulated wealth held for one year above the nisab threshold. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Zakat due on my home?
No. Your primary residence is not Zakatable. Zakat is only due on wealth that is capable of growth, and a home you live in is not considered Zakatable wealth.

How do I calculate Zakat on stocks?
Pay 2.5% of the market value of your stocks on your Zakat date. For dividend stocks, dividends received during the year are added to your cash and Zakat is calculated on the total.

Is cryptocurrency Zakatable?
Most contemporary scholars rule that cryptocurrency is Zakatable if it exceeds the nisab and has been held for one lunar year. Pay 2.5% of the market value on your Zakat date.

What is the nisab for Zakat in 2026?
The nisab is the value of 85 grams of gold or 595 grams of silver. As of 2026, 85g of gold is approximately SAR 24,000-28,000 depending on current gold prices.

Can I pay Zakat monthly instead of yearly?
Yes, many Muslims choose to pay Zakat throughout the year as their income comes in. However, the calculation should still be based on a full year of wealth accumulation.
