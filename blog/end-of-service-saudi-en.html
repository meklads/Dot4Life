End of Service Benefits in Saudi Arabia

2026-06-088 min read

More Than a Bonus, It's a Legal Right

End-of-service benefit (ESB) is not a gift from your employer — it is a right guaranteed by the Saudi Labour Law (Articles 84–88) for every worker who completes or leaves their contract, whether limited-term or indefinite. Yet most employees discover too late that they miscalculated it, or signed away part of it in a final settlement. Knowing exactly how it works could protect tens of thousands of riyals of your hard-earned income.

The benefit is payable in three main scenarios: expiry of a fixed-term contract, termination of an indefinite-term contract, and resignation (subject to conditions). In all cases, the reference is your last total wage — not your basic salary alone. This is the single most common miscalculation employees make.

The Core Formula

The standard rule under Saudi Labour Law: half a month's wage for each of the first five years, then a full month's wage for every year after five, calculated on your last total wage. Partial years are paid proportionally based on actual days worked.

Example 1: Employee with 10 years of service and a final wage of 10,000 SAR. First 5 years = 5 × (0.5 × 10,000) = 25,000 SAR. Next 5 years = 5 × (1 × 10,000) = 50,000 SAR. Total = 75,000 SAR. Note: this full amount applies only if the employer terminates the contract. In case of resignation, a reduction scale applies automatically.

Example 2 (partial year): Employee with 7 years and 4 months of service, final wage 12,000 SAR. First 5 years = 5 × 6,000 = 30,000. The 2 full years = 2 × 12,000 = 24,000. The 4-month fraction = (4/12) × 12,000 = 4,000. Total before reduction = 58,000 SAR. Estimate your exact entitlement with our salary calculator tool.

Resignation vs Termination, A Big Difference

If your employer ends the contract (without a valid legal cause such as serious misconduct), you receive the full award regardless of your years of service. If you resign, your entitlement depends on total service duration:

  • Under 2 years: No benefit is payable.
  • 2 to 5 years: One-third of the full award.
  • 5 to 10 years: Two-thirds of the full award.
  • 10 years or more: Full award, regardless of the reason for resignation.

Indefinite-term contracts (after the 2018 labour law amendments) provide resigning employees with better protection. If you have an indefinite contract, the full award is payable after 10 years even on resignation. Check your contract type through the Qiwa platform to know which category applies to you.

The Final Settlement — What's at Stake

The final settlement (or "clearance letter") is the document you sign confirming you have received all your legal entitlements. Signing it waives your right to claim any additional amounts later. Key rules to protect yourself:

  • Never sign under pressure: You have the right to request 24 hours to review the figures.
  • Request a detailed statement: Showing years of service, final wage, and the exact calculation before deductions.
  • Contact the Ministry of Human Resources: Call 19911 (unified number) to verify your entitlement if in doubt.
  • File an online complaint: Through the Qiwa platform within 12 months of the entitlement date if the benefit is not paid.

FAQ About End-of-Service Benefits

Are allowances included in the calculation?
Yes, the benefit is calculated on your last full wage, including regular allowances (transport, housing, nature of work, etc.). Temporary or task-specific allowances are not included.

Does remote work qualify for ESB?
Yes, as long as you have a registered employment contract through the Qiwa platform. Saudi labour law covers all legally documented work arrangements.

Can the benefit be forfeited for disciplinary reasons?
If an employee is dismissed following a final disciplinary ruling for gross misconduct (e.g., damaging the employer's interests), the benefit may be forfeited — but only by a labour court ruling, not by the employer's unilateral decision.

How quickly must the employer pay?
The employer must pay the end-of-service benefit within one week of the contract end date. Delays may result in penalty fines under Saudi labour regulations.

Special Cases: Job Hopping and Its Impact on ESB

One of the most common questions from employees is what happens to their end-of-service benefit when they switch companies after 3–4 years. If you resign from Company A before 2 years, you receive nothing. If you resign after 3 years, you receive one-third of the benefit for your service period at Company A. When you join Company B, your service years reset for ESB calculation purposes, though total service may count for other benefits like long-service leave in some company policies.

Important tip: Before switching jobs, calculate the ESB value you will forfeit from your current employer and compare it with the new offer. In some cases, moving after 4 years and 11 months (just before hitting the 5-year mark) may be less costly than moving right after 5 years, because the first 5 years are paid at half-rate while subsequent years are paid at full rate. This difference can cost you thousands of riyals.

What Counts as "Last Wage"? A Detailed Breakdown

The most common point of disagreement between employees and employers is the definition of "last wage." Saudi Labour Law states that the benefit is calculated based on the employee's last total wage. But what is included?

  • Basic salary: Fully included.
  • Housing allowance: Included if it is a fixed, regular allowance. If the employer provides housing in-kind (not a cash allowance), it is not included.
  • Transportation allowance: Fully included.
  • Nature of work / hazard allowance: Included if fixed and regular.
  • Leave allowance: Not included in the final wage (calculated separately upon termination).
  • Commissions and bonuses: If regular and semi-fixed, they are included. If purely performance-based and variable, this is open to interpretation and may require legal review.
  • Representation expenses: Not included as they are reimbursements, not wages.

The general rule: anything paid in exchange for work on a regular monthly basis is "wage." Incidental or reimbursement expenses (such as travel allowance, training allowance) are not wages.

How to Verify Your Benefit Calculation Step by Step

To ensure your employer calculated your end-of-service benefit correctly, follow these steps:

  • Step 1: Determine your exact start and end dates (day, month, year). Calculate total service in years, months, and days.
  • Step 2: Identify your last total wage including all regular allowances (transport, housing, nature of work).
  • Step 3: Apply the formula: first 5 years × half wage + subsequent years × full wage + partial year proportional to days.
  • Step 4: Determine the reason for contract end (resignation or termination). If the employer terminated without cause, the benefit is full and unreduced. If resignation, apply the reduction scale based on service duration.
  • Step 5: Use our salary calculator to compare your calculated figure with what your employer has offered in the final settlement.

Remember: in case of a dispute, you can file a complaint with the Labour Court within 12 months of the entitlement date. The Ministry of Human Resources provides free mediation before court proceedings.

Three Costly Mistakes to Avoid

1) Assuming the award is on basic salary only: Saudi Labour Law specifies "last wage," not "basic salary." Your last wage includes all regular allowances and fixed benefits. Ensure these are clearly documented in your contract.

2) Not documenting service dates and salary changes: Keep all appointment letters, promotion notices, salary adjustment memos, and leave balance certificates. These documents are your evidence in any future labour dispute.

3) Signing the final settlement under pressure without verification: Never sign on the same day. Take a copy of the final calculation, review it with a qualified professional, or use our financial calculator to verify the correct amount.

When to Hire an Employment Lawyer

Not every ESB dispute requires a lawyer. But in the following cases, professional legal assistance is strongly recommended: if the disputed amount exceeds 50,000 SAR, if there is disagreement about the definition of "last wage" (especially regarding allowances and bonuses), if your employment was terminated for disciplinary reasons and you wish to challenge the decision, or if 6 months have passed since your entitlement date without payment. The Ministry of Human Resources provides a free amicable settlement service through its online platform—faster and less costly than going to court. In 2025, the successful settlement rate exceeded 70% of cases referred to mediation.

Conclusion

Your end-of-service benefit is the fruit of years of work, and misunderstanding the details can cost you thousands of riyals. Keep your documents organized, understand the difference between resignation and termination, and never sign a settlement without careful review. Use our salary calculator to estimate your benefit accurately, and when in doubt, consult a legal professional or contact the Ministry of Human Resources.
