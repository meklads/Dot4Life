Rent vs Buy: Which Option Saves More Money Over 10 Years?

Real Estate / Finance · June 2026 · Comparison

The rent-vs-buy debate is one of the most consequential financial decisions a family makes. In Saudi Arabia and the Gulf, where mortgage regulations, rental laws, and property markets differ significantly from Western countries, the answer is not always straightforward.

The 10-Year Rule

Financial experts generally agree that buying becomes more advantageous than renting if you stay in the home for at least 5-7 years. Beyond 10 years, the math tilts heavily toward ownership — provided you make a sound purchase.

Scenario: Buying a SAR 800,000 Home

With a 20% down payment (SAR 160,000) and a 25-year mortgage at 4.5% interest, your monthly payment would be approximately SAR 3,550. Over 10 years, you will have paid SAR 426,000 toward the mortgage, of which approximately SAR 180,000 goes to principal (equity) and SAR 246,000 to interest. Add maintenance costs of roughly SAR 1,200 monthly, and your total housing cost is SAR 4,750/month. After 10 years, you own SAR 180,000 in equity plus any appreciation.

Scenario: Renting an Equivalent Home

Renting the same home would cost approximately SAR 3,000-3,500 monthly. Over 10 years, that is SAR 360,000-420,000 in rent — money you will never recover. However, the SAR 160,000 down payment could have been invested. If invested at a 7% annual return, it would grow to approximately SAR 315,000 over 10 years.

The Verdict at 10 Years

Buying builds equity and provides stability. Renting offers flexibility and the ability to invest the difference. For most Gulf families who plan to stay in one city for a decade or more, buying is the wealth-building choice — but only if they can afford a 20% down payment and have a stable income.
