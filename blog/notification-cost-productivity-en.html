The Cost of Notifications on Productivity

2026-06-088 min read

Salman's Story: 70 Notifications per Hour

"I thought I was an efficient multitasker, until I discovered I was wasting 3 hours every day because of notifications." That is how Salman, a 34-year-old software engineer from Riyadh, describes his relationship with his smartphone. He received an average of 70 notifications every hour from various apps: WhatsApp for work and family, Twitter for news, Instagram for friends, LinkedIn for professional opportunities, and work email. "I would open my phone to read one notification, and 20 minutes later I would find myself scrolling through apps with no purpose. At the end of the day, I felt like I had worked hard, but my actual accomplishments were minimal compared to the time I spent." The problem is that each notification disrupts your focus for an average of 23 minutes, according to a study from the University of California Irvine published in the Journal of Human Psychology. If you receive 70 notifications per hour, you are in a state of constant distraction that prevents your brain from entering the deep focus state needed for complex tasks. After Salman applied the notification management strategies we will cover in this guide, his notifications dropped from 70 to 15 per hour, and his daily productivity increased by 60 percent. He now says: "The difference is enormous. I accomplish in 4 hours what used to take me 8 hours." Salman's story is not unique, it represents millions of employees across the Arab world who lose valuable productive hours to the constant stream of notifications. The good news is that the solution is simple and available to everyone: conscious notification management.

The True Cost of Notifications on Productivity

Notifications are not innocent alerts, they have a real cost on your productivity, mental health, and family relationships. Each notification causes a focus disruption that takes 23 minutes to return to the previous concentration level, according to research published in the Journal of Human Psychology. If you receive 10 unnecessary notifications daily, you lose 230 minutes of deep focus. This means losing approximately 4 hours daily, or 20 hours weekly, or 80 hours monthly, or 960 hours annually. To put this in perspective: 960 hours a year is 40 full working days lost to unnecessary notifications. The Saudi Ministry of Health provides guidelines for healthy technology use on its official website, including recommendations for managing screen time and reducing digital distractions. The cost is not limited to time alone. Each notification also increases cortisol levels, the stress hormone, leading to chronic mental fatigue and reduced sleep quality. A Harvard University study found that the average person checks their phone 96 times daily, and 88 percent of these checks are driven by notifications rather than an actual need to communicate. Neuroscience research has shown that each notification triggers dopamine release in the brain, the same neurotransmitter associated with addiction, which explains why it is so difficult to resist opening your phone when you hear a notification sound. This biological mechanism makes notifications behave like a slot machine, making you check your phone repeatedly in search of the next reward, even when the distractions are harming your productivity.

Types of Notifications and Their Impact

Not all notifications are equal in their impact on the brain and productivity. They can be divided into three main categories based on importance and urgency. The first category is essential notifications, which require an immediate response such as calls from parents, emergency work messages, or important appointment reminders. These represent less than 5 percent of all notifications the average person receives. The second category is useful but non-urgent notifications, such as regular emails, calendar notifications, and personal reminders. These can be checked at predetermined times without needing immediate interaction. The third category is unnecessary notifications, which are the most numerous and include social media likes, entertainment app alerts, marketing offers, and app news. These represent more than 70 percent of the notifications the average person receives, and most are designed specifically to capture your attention and keep you engaged with the app for as long as possible. The World Health Organization notes in its reports on digital health the importance of managing digital time to prevent digital stress and sleep disorders associated with excessive notifications. The key is to classify your notifications into these three categories, then disable the third category completely, schedule specific times for the second category, and allow only the first category to send immediate alerts.

Practical Strategies for Managing Notifications

Here are seven proven strategies to regain control of your time and focus, applied by Salman and thousands of other users with measurable results. First, disable all unnecessary app notifications: go to your phone settings and turn off notifications for every app that does not require an immediate response. Keep them only for essential apps like calls, text messages, and important work applications. You will be surprised how many apps send notifications without a real reason. Second, use Do Not Disturb mode: activate it during work, study, and sleep hours. You can customize it to allow calls from favorite contacts only for emergencies. Third, schedule message-checking times: instead of responding to every notification immediately, set 3 specific times daily to check email and non-urgent messages, such as 10 AM, 2 PM, and 5 PM. Fourth, use digital time management tools: iOS provides Screen Time, and Android provides Digital Wellbeing, both of which help track usage and set limits for distracting apps. Apple provides a comprehensive guide to Screen Time and managing screen time on its official website, including how to set app limits and schedule downtime. Fifth, remove distracting apps from your home screen: if the app is not visible on your home screen, you will use it consciously rather than out of automatic habit. Try this simple trick for one week and notice the difference. Sixth, use notification filtering: most apps allow customization of notifications to include only important alerts. For example, you can set WhatsApp to send notifications only from individual chats and not from groups. Seventh, apply the five-minute rule: if responding to a message takes less than 5 minutes, do it immediately during your designated message time. If it takes longer, add it to your task list for later processing. Also use the Priority Matrix to classify your daily tasks by importance and urgency, focusing on what truly matters instead of reacting to every notification that appears on your screen. The most important factor is consistency: real change needs 21 days of regular practice to form a new notification management habit, so be patient with yourself.

How Notifications Affect Family Relationships

Notifications do not only affect your productivity at work, they also deeply impact your family relationships. The phenomenon of "phubbing" or phone snubbing has become an increasing social problem in Gulf and Arab societies. A 2024 study published in the Journal of Family Relations found that 60 percent of spouses feel neglected because their partner checks their phone while talking to them, and 45 percent of divorce cases in recent years have been directly or indirectly linked to smartphone addiction. Children are also negatively affected when they see their parents busy with phones instead of interacting with them. The impact of notifications on children is deeper than we think: a child who sees their parents constantly checking phones learns that technology is more important than human relationships, and their emotional attachment to their parents weakens. For this reason, notification management is not just a productivity issue but a family and parenting issue that affects the health of relationships within the family. Dedicate phone-free time with your family, such as dinner time and the hour before sleep. You can also create a "phone box" at the entrance of your home where all phones are placed during family meals. Use the Screen Time Tracker to monitor your family's technology use and make informed decisions to reduce collective screen time and increase real family interaction time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many notifications does the average person receive daily?

The average person receives between 100 and 300 notifications daily depending on their work nature and number of apps used. Studies indicate that 70 percent of these notifications are unnecessary and can be turned off without any negative impact.

What is the impact of notifications on focus?

Each notification disrupts focus for an average of 23 minutes. This means 10 unnecessary notifications cost you approximately 4 hours of deep focus daily. Returning to the original task after distraction takes longer than most people think.

How do I disable app notifications completely?

On iOS: Settings > Notifications > select the app > toggle off "Allow Notifications." On Android: Settings > Notifications > App notifications > select the app > toggle off notifications. You can also use Focus mode on iOS or Do Not Disturb on Android to manage notifications by time.

Do notifications affect sleep quality?

Yes, significantly. The blue light from screens suppresses melatonin production, the sleep hormone. Additionally, evening notifications stimulate the nervous system and keep the brain alert. It is recommended to turn off all notifications at least two hours before sleep and keep the phone in another room.

What are the best apps for notification management?

The best tools are built into the operating system itself: Screen Time for iOS and Digital Wellbeing for Android. External apps like Freedom and Forest are also useful for blocking distractions. Choose the tool that suits your phone's operating system and start applying it gradually.

Notifications are a useful tool when we use them consciously and control them, but they become a source of distraction, stress, and wasted time when we let them control our day. Regaining control of your notifications means reclaiming your time, focus, mental health, and family relationships. Start today by turning off unnecessary notifications and you will notice the difference in your productivity and quality of life within a few days. Remember that every notification you disable returns 23 minutes of focus to your day. Start with one small step: turn off notifications for one unnecessary app today, and repeat the process tomorrow with another app. Gradual change is lasting change.
