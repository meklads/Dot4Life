BMI: Why Science Is Revealing the Full Truth

June 2025·8 min read·BMI & Health

Introduction

In June 2023, the American Medical Association (AMA) made a historic decision: Body Mass Index (BMI) is no longer accurate enough to be the sole measure of health. This decision came as no surprise to researchers who have known for decades that BMI has profound limitations — especially for people of Middle Eastern, South Asian, and African descent.

In this article, we reveal why BMI is lying to you, what alternative metrics predict your health more accurately, and how you can assess your weight properly — especially if you're Saudi or from the Gulf region.

Quick Summary

The bottom line: Waist-to-Height Ratio (WHtR) outperforms BMI. The golden rule: your waist should be less than half your height. If you're 170 cm tall, your waist should be under 85 cm.

What's Wrong with BMI?

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ (height in meters)². The problem? BMI cannot distinguish between muscle and fat, and it tells us nothing about where fat is distributed in the body. Belly fat (visceral fat) is the most dangerous type, yet a muscular athlete and an obese person can have the same BMI.

More dangerous for Gulf Arabs: Research shows that Arab and Saudi populations tend to accumulate visceral belly fat even with normal BMI. This means a Saudi person with BMI 23 may have higher health risks than a Westerner with BMI 27 — because the fat is concentrated in the abdomen.

What Did the AMA Say?

In its June 2023 policy update, the AMA recommended: BMI should not be used as the sole measure for health assessment. It should be combined with other metrics like waist circumference, waist-to-height ratio, and body fat distribution. BMI has a history of racial bias as it was developed based on Western populations only. More accurate measures should be adopted in clinical diagnosis.

Waist-to-Height Ratio — The Superior Metric

WHtR outperforms BMI in predicting heart disease and type 2 diabetes risk. Studies involving over 300,000 people show WHtR is superior to BMI in predicting health risks.

How to calculate: Measure your waist at the navel, divide by your height. Example: 90 cm waist, 170 cm height → 90÷170 = 0.53.

Result: Below 0.5 = healthy, 0.5-0.6 = moderate risk, above 0.6 = high risk. Golden rule: keep your waist below half your height.

What This Means for You — Practical Guide

If your BMI is under 25: Great, but check your WHtR. If it's below 0.5, you're in an excellent health range. If it's above 0.5, focus on reducing belly fat even at a normal weight.

If your BMI is 25-30: Pay attention. WHtR is most important here. If it's below 0.5, your fat distribution is healthy. If it's above 0.6, you're at risk and should consult a doctor.

If your BMI is over 30: Both indicators signal the need for intervention. Consult a doctor for a comprehensive health plan.

Practical Tips for Better Health

Focusing on belly fat is key. Diet: reduce added sugars and refined carbs, increase protein and fiber. Physical activity: 30 minutes of walking daily + resistance training twice weekly. Sleep: 7-8 hours nightly — poor sleep is linked to increased belly fat.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is BMI inaccurate for Gulf Arabs?
BMI cannot distinguish between muscle and fat. Gulf Arabs tend to accumulate visceral belly fat even with normal BMI, hiding real health risks.

What is more accurate than BMI?
Waist-to-Height Ratio (WHtR). The rule: your waist should be less than half your height.

How do I calculate waist-to-height ratio?
Divide your waist (cm) by your height (cm). A healthy ratio is below 0.5.

Did the AMA recognize BMI's problems?
Yes, in 2023 the AMA officially recognized BMI's limitations and called for additional metrics.

What is a healthy weight range for Saudis?
Most important: WHtR below 0.5, waist under 80 cm for women and 94 cm for men.

Conclusion

BMI isn't your enemy — but it's not the whole truth either. Use it as a starting tool, but don't rely on it alone. Waist-to-Height Ratio (WHtR) is the more accurate measure and you can measure it with a simple tape measure. Remember: health isn't just a number on a scale — it's healthy weight distribution, regular physical activity, and good sleep.
